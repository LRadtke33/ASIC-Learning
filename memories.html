Lesson 43 / 84 · Part V, Digital implementation

Memories: SRAM, OpenRAM and DFFRAM

A 6T bit cell, an array, and the OpenRAM hard macros that actually ship in this sky130A PDK — with area-per-bit from the LEF, compared with a HD flop.

Where the numbers come from. Macro outlines, pin names and areas are from the LEF and Verilog of sky130_sram_macros in this course’s PDK (ciel 8afc8346a57fe1ab7934ba5a6056ea8b43078e71). Flop area is the LEF of sky130_fd_sc_hd__dfxtp_1, 7.360 × 2.720 µm = 20.0192 µm², matching the 20.02 µm² figure often quoted. No 6T butterfly (SNM) simulation was run; that plot is not shown. DFFRAM area is not measured in this edition.

What you will learn

  • Read a 6T cell and say what the word-line, bit-lines, decoders and sense amplifiers are for.
  • Use an OpenRAM 1RW+1R macro from this PDK: ports, size, and supplies.
  • Compare area per bit of a HD flop against 1 kB and 2 kB compiled SRAMs, and say why a small SRAM can lose.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
A compiled SRAM is a hard macro in the floorplan (lesson 55). March tests for it are lesson 41.
Tools
OpenRAM (the compiler that produced these macros); DFFRAM (a standard-cell memory generator). This lesson reads the macros already installed in the PDK; it does not re-run either compiler.
Why it matters
On many SoCs the memories are most of the area. Treating them as “just a bigger flop array” mis-sizes the die and mis-tests the chip.

1. A bit is a pair of inverters

To write, the word-line opens the pass gates and the bit-line drivers overpower the inverter pair. To read, the bit-lines are precharged, the word-line opens, and a sense amplifier tells which bit-line fell. Static noise margin (SNM) is the smaller of the two “squares” in a butterfly plot of the inverter transfer curves; it was not simulated here, and no SNM number is quoted. A foundry 6T cell is a carefully drawn layout (matched pull-downs, a weaker pass gate than the driver); the sketch above is only the circuit.

Around the array sit a row decoder (one WL), a column mux, precharge, write drivers and sense amplifiers. That periphery is why a small compiled SRAM can have a worse area per bit than a flop, which is what the LEF numbers in Figure 2 show.

2. An OpenRAM macro in this PDK

The installed PDK includes sky130_sram_macros. The 1 kB block the gap analysis named is present: sky130_sram_1kbyte_1rw1r_32x256_8 — 256 words of 32 bits, write granularity 8 bits (four wmask0 bits), one read-write port and one read-only port. Its LEF SIZE is 479.78 × 397.5 µm (area 190,712.55 µm²). Verilog ports, matching the LEF pins:

The behavioural Verilog that ships with the macro warns that its DELAY and T_HOLD parameters are arbitrary — it is not a Liberty-accurate timing model. For STA you use the compiled Liberty (sky130_sram_1kbyte_1rw1r_32x256_8_TT_1p8V_25C.lib is in the same PDK). In the floorplan the block is a CLASS BLOCK hard macro: you place it, hook vccd1/vssd1 to the grid, and keep a halo for the pins, Pins in this LEF use met3 and met4; the data and address ports sit along the bottom edge (RECT y = 0).

Two other OpenRAM macros are in the same LEF directory: sky130_sram_1kbyte_1rw1r_8x1024_8 (455.30 × 446.46 µm) and sky130_sram_2kbyte_1rw1r_32x512_8 (683.10 × 416.54 µm).

The 2 kB macro is already cheaper per bit than a flop; the 1 kB macros are not. That is the periphery talking. It is also why “replace every register file with SRAM” is not automatic at these depths. A 64-bit register is still a row of flops.

4. DFFRAM, without a number we did not measure

DFFRAM (AUCOHL) is a standard-cell memory: the bits are latches or flops from the library, compiled as a placeable macro, so it goes through the same LibreLane / OpenROAD path as logic. Latch-based configurations are used on SKY130; this course does not re-run the generator and does not quote an area-per-bit for it. The comparison you can make today is Figure 2: a raw dfxtp_1 versus the OpenRAM LEFs. A DFFRAM measurement would be a later lab, with the generator’s output placed and its LEF SIZE divided by depth×width, on the same PDK revision.

5. Testing the array

A March C− test, and why a memory BIST sits next to the macro, is lesson 41. The OpenRAM behavioural model is not a substitute for that test: it is a simulation stub with an arbitrary delay.

Why can a 1 kB SRAM be larger, per bit, than a flip-flop, while a 2 kB SRAM on the same compiler is smaller?

The decoders, precharge, sense amplifiers and wells are paid once per macro. At 8,192 bits that tax is still bigger, in this PDK’s OpenRAM 1 kB LEFs, than 20.02 µm². At 16,384 bits the same kind of periphery is amortised and the 2 kB 32×512 LEF falls to 17.37 µm²/bit. The numbers are those three LEF outlines, not a general SRAM scaling law.
