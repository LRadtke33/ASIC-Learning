Lesson 54 / 84 · Part VI, Physical cells and the floorplan

Level Shifters

A signal crossing supply domains needs a verified interface: compatible logic thresholds and device stress limits, or an appropriate level shifter. This library has two different shifters. They are not the same voltage.

Where the numbers come from. The HD cell is lpflow_lsbuf_lh_isowell_tap_1: LEF, schematic spice, and tt_025C_1v80. The high-voltage cells are sky130_fd_sc_hvl__lsbufhv2lv_1 and lsbuflv2hv_1: LEF and schematic spice. HVL delays are not quoted here; they come from the HVL library’s own Liberty files. The 5 V note is the foundry library page.

What you will learn

  • Decide when a signal crossing supply domains needs a level shifter.
  • Compare the SKY130 HD shifter with the HVL cells that cross between 5 V and 1.8 V devices.
  • Keep voltage translation, isolation and clock-domain crossing as separate checks.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

Why it matters
A signal crossing between supply voltages can be misread, or can overstress the receiving transistors.

1. Two supplies, one buffer

A source output swing may not satisfy the destination’s input-high threshold, or may exceed its allowed terminal voltage. A level shifter is designed for a specified pair of supply domains. Device connections vary by architecture; not every input device is simply powered by the source rail. The cells discussed here preserve the logic value while changing the output voltage domain.

The foundry note says the HD library provides multi-voltage cells, and that the high-voltage library, sky130_fd_sc_hvl, is the one with 5 V devices. Keep those apart. The HD shifter below stays inside the 1.8 V device family. The HVL shifters are the ones that use the 5 V transistors.

2. The HD shifter is also a well tap

sky130_fd_sc_hd__lpflow_lsbuf_lh_isowell_tap_1 is class CORE WELLTAP, site name unithd, SIZE 6.440 BY 5.440. Width is 14 sites of 0.46 µm. Height is 5.44 µm, exactly two row heights, even though the site name in the LEF is the single-height site. Area 35.0336 µm². Leakage 0.008449956 nW.

Liberty marks is_level_shifter true and level_shifter_type LH. The recorded input and output voltage ranges are each 1.2–2.1 V. Those metadata ranges do not validate every input/output rail combination or power-off sequence. In the particular TT file used here, the voltage map sets both LOWLVPWR and VPWR to 1.8 V. A real low-to-high operating point needs matching multi-supply characterization and voltage-stress checks. Pin A is 6.005 fF; X has function (A) and a 128.184 fF maximum load in that file. LEF pins are A, X, LOWLVPWR, VGND and VPWR; the schematic also exposes VPB.

Every device is nfet_01v8 or pfet_01v8_hvt, length 0.15 µm. Eleven NMOS, five PMOS. The output pull-up is a high-Vt PMOS, 0.79 µm wide, sitting on VPWR. The input PMOS whose gate is A is 1.0 µm wide and sits on LOWLVPWR. This cell shifts between two supplies inside the HD voltage family. It is not a 5 V shifter. It is also a well tap (lesson 48), which is why the class says so.

3. The HVL cells cross between the 5 V devices and the 1.8 V devices

The foundry page gives the HVL single-row site as 0.48 × 4.07 µm, 14 met1 tracks, and says the library's timing models are valid from 1.65 V to 5.5 V. The low-to-high level shifter is described as functional from 1.2 V to 5.5 V. The two cells below are drawn on unithvdbl, which the LEF defines as 0.480 by 8.140 µm: two of those 4.07 µm rows. 4.07 × 2 = 8.14.

lsbufhv2lv_1 is high to low: pin A is on the g5v0d10v5 pair that connects to VPWR and VGND, and pin X is on the 1.8 V devices powered from LVPWR. lsbuflv2hv_1 is the other direction. Both LEFs list A, X, LVPWR, VGND, VNB, VPB, and VPWR. Their timing is in the HVL Liberty data for each supply pair. Compare these with about 0.1 ns for an HD buffer (lesson 35): one crossing onto the 5 V devices costs about five to fourteen HD buffer delays, depending on the edge. The two numbers above are one corner. The low-to-high cell is slowest when LVPWR is low and VPWR is high, so read the corner that matches your supplies.

Antenna on the HD input is gate area 0.603 µm². The HVL high-to-low input is gate area 0.420 µm², and the low-to-high input is 0.252 µm². Output diffusion area is 0.4025, 0.4929, and 0.59625 µm² on the HD cell, the high-to-low cell, and the low-to-high cell. The library also lists _2 and _4 variants of the HD shifter.

A floorplan that places the HD shifter has to give it two rows of height (lesson 55). A floorplan that places the HVL cell is on a different site, 0.48 µm wide and 8.14 µm tall for the double-height cell, and it does not drop onto an HD row.

4. A common low-to-high circuit uses a cross-coupled pair

A common low-to-high topology uses a cross-coupled PMOS pair on the high rail and lower-side controlled pull-downs. Regeneration restores a full swing, but contention, input overdrive, device stress and sizing determine whether it switches correctly. This concept does not describe every high-to-low shifter, and figure 2 is not a transistor trace of either referenced cell.

The HD cell's Liberty range is 1.2 V to 2.1 V on both sides, and its transistors are the 1.8 V devices. The HVL cells are the ones that use the 5 V devices. The drawing is the usual circuit. It is not a trace of either SPICE netlist, and it has no delay.

5. Voltage translation, isolation and CDC are separate checks

ProblemRequired functionWhat it does not guarantee
1.2 V source cannot meet destination VIHLevel translationSafe behavior with either rail off
Powered-down block drives an active blockIsolation and defined clamp sequencingVoltage stress safety unless explicitly designed for it
Unrelated source and destination clocksCDC protocol/synchronizationLevel shifting or metastability-free sampling

Worked example (assumed thresholds). If a 1.8 V receiver requires VIH ≥ 0.7×VDD = 1.26 V and the source guarantees only 1.10 V high, the margin is 1.10−1.26 = −0.16 V. Translation is needed unless a different qualified receiver is selected. The 0.7 ratio and 1.10 V source are teaching assumptions, not published SKY130 input limits.

Check supply ramps, rail order, isolation enable timing, body connections, leakage/back-powering, and every VGS, VGD, VDS limit in the selected PDK. A translator with valid logic function at nominal supply is not automatically power-off tolerant.

Checkpoint: does a level shifter replace a synchronizer?

No. It changes the voltage domain. A signal with an unrelated timing domain still needs an appropriate CDC scheme. A powered-down source may also require isolation.

Check your understanding — a 1.8 V output drives an input in a 3.3 V domain directly. What can go wrong?

A 1.8 V high can sit below the receiver’s input-high threshold, so it is not a valid 1. The receiving PMOS may not turn fully off, letting current flow through both transistors. A low-to-high level shifter restores a full 3.3 V swing.

In the open SKY130 flow. Commercial flows describe supply domains, level shifters and isolation in a power-intent file (UPF), which synthesis and place-and-route use to insert the cells and which dedicated checkers verify. LibreLane’s default flow takes no power-intent file: shifters are instantiated in the RTL or netlist, and each domain’s supplies are connected explicitly.

Key takeaways

  • A level shifter must be characterized for its source/destination rails and device limits. The function of these examples is a buffer.
  • The HD cell is 6.44 × 5.44 µm, class WELLTAP, Liberty range 1.2–2.1 V, all 1.8 V transistors.
  • It has pins LOWLVPWR and VPWR. A is 6.005 fF. X max is 128.184 fF.
  • lsbufhv2lv_1 is 8.16 × 8.14 µm, high side in, 1.8 V side out. lsbuflv2hv_1 is 10.56 × 8.14 µm, the other way.
  • The 5 V devices are g5v0d10v5. The HD shifter does not use them.
  • A cross-coupled pair is a common low-to-high topology, not a universal model for every direction or cell. HD metadata records 1.2–2.1 V; each operating combination still needs validation. HVL uses a different device family.
