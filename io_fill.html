Lesson 66 / 84 · Part VII, The chip boundary

I/O Fill

A pad-ring gap needs a pad-ring structure. Core fillers, I/O bus fillers, metal-density fill, and intentional disconnects have different electrical jobs.

What you will learn

  • Explain why a gap in the pad ring needs a pad-ring filler, not a core filler.
  • Tile a pad-ring gap with the 1, 5, 10 and 20 µm extension fillers.
  • Keep fillers, overlays and intentional bus disconnects in their separate roles.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
Pad-ring assembly in step 4, floorplan and power grid.
Tools
The pad-ring generator — Caravel’s, or OpenROAD’s in LibreLane’s Chip flow — places the filler cells between pads.
Why it matters
The ring’s supply buses must stay continuous all the way around the die.

The original upstream sky130_fd_io cell subset and the sky130_ef_io extensions are different packages. The extension supplied by open-pdks includes real pad-bus fillers.

1. Core fill does not continue a pad bus

The HD core fillers fill_1, fill_2, fill_4, and fill_8 fit a 2.72 µm row on a 0.46 µm site. They continue the standard-cell template, including the cell rails. They do not carry the many met4/met5 supply and analog buses in a roughly 200 µm-tall I/O ring.

Neighboring compatible pad cells connect where their edge pins abut. A gap interrupts that direct bus path. A complete ring can sometimes still connect electrically around the other side, but the missing segment changes resistance, ESD paths, and local current crowding. Do not mistake “the nets still have the same name” for a complete and legal ring.

2. SKY130 extension fillers are available

The open-pdks sky130_ef_io configuration explicitly lists common-bus slice masters as PAD_FILLERS. Their checked LEFs declare CLASS PAD SPACER. The actual outlines are:

The 197.965 µm abstract height should not be rounded into a placement rule. These cells belong to a particular compatible I/O kit and orientation scheme. Verify bus coordinates, origin, wrapper choice, and approved abutments when assembling the ring. Similar heights and matching labels are necessary clues, not proof of compatibility.

3. Worked example: tile a legal 37 µm gap

Assume the selected kit, orientation, and placement grid permit a 37 µm straight gap. One width decomposition is 20 + 10 + 5 + 1 + 1 = 37 µm. This uses five bus slices and closes the width exactly. The arithmetic is useful, but layout must still show the intended bus shapes meeting without a gap or a short to another domain.

4. An overlay or a disconnect is a different object

The upstream overlay_vddio_hvc and overlay_vccd_lvc examples occupy complete pad slots: 75 × 200 µm and 75 × 198 µm respectively. They are not arbitrary-width slivers. A disconnect slice intentionally breaks selected buses between voltage domains. Replacing it with a common-bus filler may short supplies that were intended to remain separate.

Metal-density fill is another separate operation. Its job is process density and uniformity; it cannot be used to promise functional continuity of a pad supply or ESD bus. Never bridge a ring gap by treating random fill as a supply connection.

Analog ↔ digital: the common I/O bus may include analog multiplexing paths as well as digital supplies. Filling a gap must preserve the intended isolation and reference return path, not just make the ring look continuous. Run connectivity/LVS and applicable DRC after assembly, and include the final bus path in EM/IR and ESD review.

Check your understanding — May a common-bus filler replace a domain disconnect?

No. A filler continues buses; a disconnect intentionally separates selected ones. Substituting the filler can connect different supplies or analog buses together.

Check your understanding — tile a 46 µm pad-ring gap with the fewest fillers.

20 + 20 + 5 + 1 = 46 µm: four slices. As in the 37 µm example, each filler must also match its neighbors’ bus geometry and voltage domain, not just the width.

Key takeaways

  • Core fill and pad fill are different physical structures.
  • The upstream fd_io subset does not describe the complete installed I/O kit; ef_io provides common-bus fillers.
  • The checked slice widths are 1, 5, 10, and 20 µm, with LEF height 197.965 µm.
  • Choose fillers by compatible bus geometry, orientation, and voltage domain, not width alone.
  • Intentional disconnects, overlays, and metal-density fill must keep their separate roles.
