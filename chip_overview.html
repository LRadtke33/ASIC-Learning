What Is a Chip? Board, Package & Die

The part you hold is three nested objects: a silicon die, a package that protects and connects it, and the circuit board it is soldered to. This lesson names each level and puts real SKY130 sizes on it.

Reading the evidence. Dimensions labeled Caravel come from the published Caravel harness drawings and design files for the SKY130 shuttle chip. Zoom drawings are teaching diagrams; each states its own scale. Sources are listed at the end.

  • Tell a die from a package, and a package pin from an on-chip pad.

Why it matters

1. A chip is a packaged die

An integrated circuit (IC) is a complete circuit — transistors, resistors, capacitors and the wires between them — manufactured together on one piece of silicon. That piece of silicon is the die (plural dies or dice). A finished die is a brittle rectangle a few millimeters on a side. Its connection points are tens of micrometers wide and far too close together to solder by hand.

So the die is sealed inside a package: a protective body with metal leads or solder balls spaced widely enough for assembly. The package is soldered to a printed circuit board (PCB), which carries power and signals between all the parts of a product. In everyday speech, “chip” means the packaged part you see on a board. In this course, die means the silicon itself, package means everything wrapped around it, and board means the PCB.

An ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) is an IC designed for one product or job. It contrasts with general-purpose parts such as a microcontroller, or an FPGA (field-programmable gate array), whose logic is configured after manufacture. This course is about designing the die of an ASIC. Every decision on the die still has to work through the package and the board, which is why Part I starts outside the silicon.

From the board to the transistor gate the length scale shrinks by roughly six orders of magnitude: 100 mm divided by 0.15 µm is about 670,000. Each level was designed by different people with different tools, and the interfaces between levels are where many real problems hide.

3. How the levels connect

Every joint in Figure 2 is part of the circuit. The solder joint, the package lead, the bond wire, the pad and the on-chip metal each have resistance, capacitance and inductance. A signal leaving a transistor crosses all of them, and so does the current that powers the transistor.

That current also has to come back. Every supply connection is half of a loop. The positive supply is conventionally called VDD and the ground (reference) rail VSS; SKY130 standard cells call the same two pins VPWR and VGND (lesson 4 explains their two extra body pins). Current leaves through the power pins and returns through the ground pins. A drawing that shows only the signal wire hides half of the circuit. Later lessons put numbers on this loop: the I/O ring (lesson 64), supply drop (lesson 68) and package coupling (lesson 69).

Check your understanding — a signal leaves a transistor on the die and reaches a pin of another chip. Which physical structures does it pass through?

Transistor drain → local interconnect and metal layers → the I/O pad cell, with its output driver and electrostatic-discharge (ESD) protection, → the pad → a bond wire or solder bump → the package lead or ball → a solder joint → a PCB trace → the other chip’s package, and in again. The return current flows back through the ground connections of both chips and the board’s ground plane.

4. Foundry, PDK and tapeout

A foundry manufactures chips designed by other companies; the factory is a fab (fabrication plant). SkyWater Technology runs a 200 mm wafer fab in Bloomington, Minnesota. Its 130 nm mixed-signal CMOS (complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor) process grew out of the Cypress process called S8, and it is the process the SKY130 design kit describes.

A PDK (process design kit) is the foundry’s description of a process for designers: transistor models for circuit simulation, the geometric rules every layout must obey, and libraries of pre-designed cells. Google and SkyWater released the SKY130 PDK as open source in 2020, which is why this course can quote real files instead of invented ones.

Tapeout is the moment a finished layout database is sent to the foundry for manufacture. The name dates from when that database was delivered on magnetic tape. Lesson 8 walks through the design flow that ends there.

Many SKY130 chips use Caravel, a ready-made chip “harness” created by Efabless: a fixed ring of pads, a small RISC-V management processor and a 2.92 × 3.52 mm user area for your own design. Efabless shut down in early 2025. ChipFoundry acquired its assets and restarted Caravel-based SKY130 shuttles, and other SKY130 shuttle services have appeared since. Check the current provider and its rules before planning a design.

6. Worked example: how much is on a die?

The Caravel die is 3.60 × 5.20 mm = 18.72 mm², or 18.72 million µm². The smallest SKY130 HD inverter, inv_1, occupies 1.38 × 2.72 µm = 3.7536 µm². Dividing gives about 4.99 million inverter footprints.

That number is a geometric upper bound, not a capacity. It ignores the pad ring, the power grid, routing space, clock buffers and the management processor. Real designs also leave a large fraction of placement sites empty so that the wiring fits (lesson 55 discusses utilization). The useful lesson is the ratio: one die holds millions of cells, and each cell holds a handful of transistors.

Check your understanding — is the black rectangle soldered to a board the die?

No. It is the package: usually epoxy mold compound around a metal lead frame. Caravel’s die (3.60 × 5.20 mm) sits inside a 9 × 9 mm QFN body. In a wafer-level chip-scale package (WLCSP) the package is almost exactly the size of the die, with solder balls on its face (lesson 6).

Key takeaways

  • A die is the silicon; a package protects it and spreads its connections out; a PCB connects packages.
  • Caravel example: 9 × 9 mm QFN-64 package, 3.60 × 5.20 mm die, 2.92 × 3.52 mm user area, 2.72 µm cell rows, 0.15 µm gate length.
  • Every joint between levels has resistance, capacitance and inductance, and every supply connection is half of a current loop.
  • A foundry builds the wafers; a PDK describes its process; tapeout hands over the finished layout.
  • ASIC means application-specific integrated circuit: a die designed for one job.
