The Cell Template: Tracks, Sites & Edges

Where row height and site width come from, and how compatible cell-edge rules support legal abutment.

What you will learn

  • Explain how a library template fixes row height, site width and the cell-edge rules.
  • Count the HD row height in routing tracks: 8 × 0.34 µm = 2.72 µm.
  • Separate the placement site, the routing tracks and the 0.005 µm manufacturing grid.

SKY130 data and teaching models. The SKY130 facts in this lesson are the 0.46 × 2.72 µm unithd site (and the 0.46 × 5.44 µm unithddbl), the 0.34 µm met1 pitch with its 0.17 µm offset, the li1 pitches, the 0.48 µm rails and the 0.005 µm manufacturing grid. The 9-line template, the 6T/9T/12T height families, the regular-gate pattern and the half-rule edge contract are teaching models of how libraries are designed. Real libraries set their own conventions, and SKY130’s internal poly and diffusion geometry has to be read from its layout and rules.

1. The template comes first

A library architecture defines related choices such as row height, site width, rail positions and well boundaries. Ordinary cells in a compatible family follow that template; special, multiheight and other library families may use different frames or edge restrictions. This lesson explains those choices and separates a generic template from the measured HD examples.

2. Row height is counted in routing tracks

A library’s track count describes its cell height in units of a routing pitch, but libraries count differently: some count track centerlines, some count pitch intervals, some use another reference layer. A “9-track” label does not always mean the height is nine router pitches. For SKY130 HD the published height is 2.72 µm and the met1 pitch is 0.34 µm: eight pitch intervals. Section 5 separates its nine boundary-to-boundary centerlines from its eight offset routing tracks.

  • Boundary-centered rails extend into both adjacent rows and take part of the potential signal-routing region; spacing, pins and obstructions reduce the usable capacity further.
  • Compatible cells use well and implant boundaries that meet their abutment rules; matching one drawn boundary says nothing about a different cell family or voltage domain.
  • The remaining active-region space limits the width of an individual device finger. Drive also depends on device type, length, bias, folding and internal wiring.

3. Site width, contacted gate pitch, and library choice

The site width is chosen so that cells repeat and abut legally. It is often related to the contacted gate pitch (CPP), but it does not have to equal one CPP. Some restricted-layout processes impose a regular pattern of gates and dummy gates, as Figure 3 illustrates. For SKY130 the established facts are the 0.46 µm site and the matching li1 x pitch.

4. The edge contract

Abutment works only for compatible cells in legal orientations. The library, PDK and placement tool can require edge classes, spacing, padding or special boundary cells, so not every cell may sit beside every other. The simple edge contract below shows one way a library can make common pairs abut cleanly:

  • Half-rule spacing: if a symmetric rule requires separation s across the boundary, setbacks of at least s/2 on both sides provide s. Context-dependent or asymmetric rules need different treatment.
  • Compatible merge layers: selected rail, well and implant shapes run to common boundary positions. Whether shapes may merge depends on their nets, voltage domains and layer rules.
  • Boundary gate patterns, where required: their ownership and spacing are specific to the process and library; a shared half-in, half-out dummy strip is one convention among several.

The architecture also defines legal row boundaries and filler choices. Endcap/boundary cells can terminate required well, implant or gate patterns; compatible fillers can continue selected shapes through a gap. Which layers need continuity, and which cell pairings are allowed, must be established by that library and the physical-design flow.

5. The SkyWater template, in micrometers

unithd fixes both numbers this lesson is about. Site width 0.46 µm. Row height 2.72 µm. met1 pitch is 0.34 µm, and 2.72 / 0.34 = 8 exactly, so the row is eight pitches tall. Counting both rail centers and stepping by 0.34 µm gives nine lines: 0, 0.34, 0.68, 1.02, 1.36, 1.70, 2.04, 2.38, 2.72. This is one way to visualize the published 9-track label; it does not make the generic Figure 1 a measured SKY130 transistor template.

With the tech LEF’s PITCH 0.34 and OFFSET 0.17 and the row origin drawn here, track centers fall at 0.17, 0.51, 0.85, 1.19, 1.53, 1.87, 2.21 and 2.55 µm: eight nominal tracks. The first and last lie inside the 0.48 µm boundary rails, leaving six outside the rail rectangles, and spacing, pins and obstructions constrain those further. A real implementation can redefine its TRACKS, so check them rather than assuming the tech-LEF default phase.

The li1 pitches are 0.46 µm in x and 0.34 µm in y. Matching the x pitch to the site width helps repeated cell geometry line up, though it does not by itself guarantee a legal pin landing. unithddbl is 0.46 × 5.44 µm for compatible double-height cells; the ordinary cells in this lesson use the 2.72 µm HD height.

The rail rectangle is identical on inv_1, nand2_1, fill_1, decap_4, and dfxtp_1: met1 from y = −0.24 to 0.24, and from y = 2.48 to 2.96, across the full width of that cell. Abutment is those rectangles landing on the same coordinates. The shared stripe is 0.48 µm wide in the current-flow sense. Lesson 30 multiplies that width by the met1 current limit.

The site width is what the placer snaps to. The LEF does not state a poly pitch; that would have to be measured from the cell layout. The LEF fact is that the site and the li1 x pitch are the same 0.46 µm. Section 3 explains one possible relationship to gate pitch; equality has not been established for this library by the LEF. The GDS is the place to check the poly grating itself.

6. Coordinates land on the manufacturing grid

The tech LEF opens with MANUFACTURINGGRID 0.005. A coordinate in this process is an integer number of 0.005 µm steps, which is 5 nm.

The template numbers already land on that grid. Site width 0.46 µm is 92 steps. Row height 2.72 µm is 544 steps. met1 pitch 0.34 µm is 68 steps. A rectangle whose edge falls between two ticks is off the grid, and the DRC that checks the grid will see it before any neighbor does.

Worked checkpoint — Nine lines, eight tracks, or six free routes?

Using the displayed row origin, centers y = 0.17 + n × 0.34 for n = 0…7 give eight nominal tracks. Centers at 0.17 and 2.55 µm fall inside the rails, leaving six centers outside the rail rectangles. That is not six guaranteed free routes: the clearance to rails, pin shapes, vias and other obstructions must still be checked. Nine reference lines from 0 through 2.72 µm delimit eight pitch intervals.

Check your understanding — a shape edge sits at 1.2375 µm. Is it on the SKY130 manufacturing grid?

No. The grid is 0.005 µm, and 1.2375 / 0.005 = 247.5 is not a whole number; 1.235 µm or 1.240 µm would be on grid. Being on the manufacturing grid says nothing about being on a routing track or a placement site.

Key takeaways

  • A template coordinates compatible site, rail, well and boundary geometry; special cells and other families may add restrictions.
  • Track names are conventions. Use the actual dimensions, routing pitch, grid phase and obstructions to assess capacity.
  • Shorter cells can save outline area while tightening device and pin-access space; taller cells are not automatically faster.
  • Site width may relate to contacted gate pitch, but the relationship and dummy-gate pattern require technology-specific evidence.
  • Edge contracts such as half-rule setbacks and shared boundary poly are library conventions; check the permitted cell pairs and contextual rules for the library you use.
  • The HD row is 8 × 0.34 µm = 2.72 µm tall. The offset grid has eight track centers, two of them inside the rails; the other six still need routing checks.
  • The technology LEF declares a 0.005 µm manufacturing grid, distinct from the placement and preferred-routing grids.
