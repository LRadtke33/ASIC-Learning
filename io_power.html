Lesson 68 / 84 · Part VII, The chip boundary

I/O Power and IR Drop

A power net is a resistive network with distributed loads, package connections, and a return path. Its current is not the same in every segment.

Where the numbers come from. SKY130’s LEF supplies useful geometry and sheet-resistance examples. It does not expose enough internal pad-bus geometry to derive an exact resistance per I/O cell from a side pin alone.

Model assumptions in this lesson. The full-width uniform strip and its 0.151 Ω segments are a teaching geometry, not an extracted GPIO-cell resistance. The 10 mA loads, the 0.4 Ω and 0.2 Ω path resistances and the 1 nH package inductance are example values. A real supply path needs extraction or a characterized pad-bus model, and its resistance temperature comes from the resistance model — not from the 90 °C condition attached to the electromigration limits. The I/O guide’s “up to 1 mA” remark concerns the analog-mux path, not a VDDIO supply current [AMUX usage note].

What you will learn

  • Treat the pad ring’s supplies as resistive networks with distributed loads.
  • Use R = R□ × L/W only for a defined conductor and current direction.
  • Include the return path and fast current changes, not only static voltage drop.

Why it matters
Voltage lost in the I/O supply network slows the pads and couples noise into sensitive circuits.

1. The pad ring carries several independent supplies

The core mesh receives power through the appropriate core power pads or bump connections. In sky130_fd_io, the I/O rail VDDIO, core rail VCCD, analog rail VDDA, and their returns have different purposes. Do not connect a 1.8 V core grid directly to a higher-voltage VDDIO ring just because both are “power.” [I/O supply architecture]

In the checked GPIO LEF, a tall VDDIO access rectangle is 1.27 µm along x and 24.965 µm along y. It appears at each side edge so compatible adjacent pads can abut. The rectangle does not describe the whole internal route across the 80 µm cell. An OBS rectangle is a routing blockage, not an extracted conductor annotated with a supply net.

2. Sheet-resistance arithmetic needs the actual current path

For a uniform rectangular film with current flowing along its length L, the first estimate is R = R□ L/W. The HD routing LEF gives met4 R□ = 0.047 Ω/square. [technology LEF]

GeometryAssumptionsSheet estimate
1.27 × 24.965 µm access rectangleUniform flow across x; full-width equipotential ends0.047 × 1.27 / 24.965 ≈ 2.39 mΩ
80 × 24.965 µm stripHypothetical uniform met4 route across one cell width0.047 × 80 / 24.965 ≈ 0.151 Ω
Actual GPIO supply pathNeeds internal shapes, vias, parallel metal, and loadingNot derivable from those two pin rectangles

At 10 mA the 0.151 Ω strip drops 1.51 mV. Parallel met5 lowers the resistance only where it is connected through an adequate via path, while narrow necks, detours, contacts and unequal current sharing raise local resistance and stress.

3. Worked example: distributed loads change the answer

Take a single-ended chain of ten identical 0.151 Ω segments, fed by an ideal source at the left end, with an ideal return path. Compare three loading patterns:

Loading patternSegment currentsDrop at the far end
One 1 mA load at the far end1 mA in all ten10 × 0.151 × 1 mA = 1.51 mV
One 10 mA load at the far end10 mA in all ten15.1 mV
Ten 1 mA loads, one after every segment10, 9, …, 1 mA0.151 × 1 mA × (10+…+1) = 8.305 mV

The last case uses N(N+1)/2 = 55. Multiplying every segment by the same current would be wrong for this distributed load. A closed ring fed at several points is a mesh: solve the network to find each segment’s current. Adding feeds or parallel routes can reduce drop, but the reduction depends on where the sources and loads sit.

4. Include the return path and fast current changes

If the supply path has 0.4 Ω and the return path has 0.2 Ω, a 10 mA load sees 10 mA × (0.4 + 0.2) Ω = 6 mV less rail-to-rail voltage. For a remote ideal 1.8 V source, its local rails are 1.796 V and 0.002 V, giving VDDlocal − VSSlocal = 1.794 V. Calling ground “0” in the schematic does not remove its physical voltage rise.

Static IR drop is only one part of supply integrity. A 1 nH package connection with di/dt = 20 mA/ns contributes 20 mV. Decoupling, ESR, ESL, package/board return paths, and switching correlation determine the transient response. A static PDN solver answers a DC operating-point question; it does not by itself simulate this fast transient. [OpenROAD static IR analysis]

EM remains a separate check. The quoted 90°C met4 limits in lesson 30 apply to a specified wire width and waveform. You cannot certify the entire hidden I/O bus by multiplying a side-pin height by one per-width limit. Every critical conductor and via must carry its actual share of current.

Analog ↔ digital: an analog reference error can appear long before a digital circuit stops functioning. Conversely, a rail dip changes delay and can create a timing failure at a remote register. Review the voltage at the load relative to its local return, along with the activity scenario and temperature.

Check your understanding — Why is the drop different for ten distributed loads and one far-end load with the same total current?

A distributed load removes current at each tap, so later segments carry less current. A single far-end load sends the full total through every segment. Voltage drop is the sum of each segment’s own I × R.

Check your understanding — a met4 bus 1,000 µm long and 20 µm wide carries 10 mA to a load at its far end. What is its voltage drop?

With 0.047 Ω/□ from the HD tech LEF, R = 0.047 × 1000/20 = 2.35 Ω, so the drop is 23.5 mV, and the ground return adds its own. If the same 10 mA were drawn evenly along the bus, the drop at the far end would be about half.

In the open SKY130 flow. OpenROAD’s analyze_power_grid solves the supply network for the static voltage at every node and, with -enable_em, reports the current in each grid segment; LibreLane runs it as IRDropReport. It is a static check, so package inductance and fast current steps still need a transient model. Commercial tools for both are Cadence Voltus and Ansys RedHawk.

analyze_power_grid -net VPWR
analyze_power_grid -net VPWR -enable_em

Rings around macros. The same arithmetic sizes the ring around a dropped-in macro. With the block’s current drawn along the ring and N feeds, the worst drop grows with the square of the feed spacing, so adding feeds helps faster than widening the ring (lesson 50, section 7).

Core-grid IR signoff, static versus dynamic drop, and LibreLane’s IR step are lesson 63. This lesson stays with the pad-ring supplies.

Key takeaways

  • A LEF access pin is not a complete extracted supply path.
  • Use R□L/W only for a clearly defined conductor and stated current direction.
  • Supply segment currents depend on source and load placement; the return path contributes too.
  • The guide’s 1 mA AMUX note is not a VDDIO supply-current specification.
  • Static IR, dynamic supply noise, and EM need related but distinct analyses.
