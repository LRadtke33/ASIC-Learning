Spare Cells and ECO

An engineering change after the base layers are frozen has to be made in metal. A spare cell is real gates, placed in advance, so that metal change has something to reconnect. A fill cell is not that.

Where the numbers come from. The cell is macro_sparecell. Outline and pins are the LEF. The bundle is the schematic spice. Function and capacitance are tt_025C_1v80.

What you will learn

  • Explain why a metal-only change needs spare gates placed in advance.
  • Read SKY130’s macro_sparecell: 29 sites holding NAND, NOR, inverter and tie cells.
  • Check a change electrically — load, timing, antenna — not only logically.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
Spare cells are placed in step 5, placement. They are used after step 9, physical verification, or after first silicon, when a fix may change only metal.
Tools
LibreLane provides ECO steps for small layout edits. Commercial: the ECO features of Innovus or IC Compiler II, with Conformal ECO.
Why it matters
Spare cells let a bug be fixed with a few new metal masks instead of a whole new mask set.

1. A metal-only ECO needs devices already in place

An engineering change order (ECO) is a design modification; it can occur before or after layout, and is not inherently metal-only. In a metal-only ECO, the allowed base-layer masks are frozen. Existing spare gates can be reconnected using the permitted interconnect masks. Exactly which contact/via/metal layers may change is a manufacturing and project constraint; an empty site does not create new transistors.

Empty sites filled by fill_1 (lesson 47) are not spare gates. Those cells have no transistors. A gap left on purpose is room. It becomes a gate only if a gate was placed there.

2. macro_sparecell is 29 sites

sky130_fd_sc_hd__macro_sparecell is a class CORE cell on the unithd site with SIZE 13.340 BY 2.720: 13.34 / 0.46 = 29 sites, or 36.2848 µm². Its typical-corner leakage is 0.04487112 nW, and its footprint name is sky130_fd_sc_hd__sparecell.

The only signal pin is LO, direction output, function 0, max capacitance 1.8948 pF. ANTENNAGATEAREA on that pin is 1.980 µm². That is eight times the 0.2475 µm² gate of inv_1. The pin is on li1 and on a met1 strap. Supplies are VGND and VPWR. OBS is mcon and met1. There is no met2 in the OBS.

The published macro netlist lists two nand2_2, two nor2_2, two inv_2 and one conb_1. Its positional instance ordering is not directly consistent with the separately published child .subckt declarations. Therefore the instance inventory is useful evidence, but that hierarchical file is not treated here as a verified runnable simulation deck. Resolve the ordering or use a validated extracted netlist before simulating or reusing the hidden gates.

An ECO that uses the hidden gates edits the layout, where the transistors already exist. It does not ask this LEF abstract for a NAND input. The abstract does not have one. Leaving a region of fill_* and calling it spare logic will not give you those transistors later.

3. The change is a metal strap onto a cell that is already there

An ECO that may only move metal needs the transistors already placed, with their inputs held by conb_1 so the unused gates are not floating. The conceptual edit disconnects a tie and routes a new input/output using only the permitted layers. It must not leave any old output shorted to the new driver or any unused input floating.

A fill cell cannot take the same strap and become a gate. Fill has rails and no devices (lesson 47). The spare cell is the one that already contains the NAND, NOR, and inverter.

4. Check the ECO electrically, not only logically

For an illustrative metal-only fix, suppose a spare gate adds 80 ps of logic delay and its detour adds 40 ps of extracted wire delay. A path with 100 ps of setup margin now has 100−80−40 = −20 ps slack, even if the Boolean expression is correct. The numbers are assumed; actual values require the chosen cell, slew, load and extracted route.

CheckReason
Logical equivalence and testThe new connection must implement the intended behavior in every mode
DRC and LVSPermitted masks must produce legal geometry and correct connectivity
Timing, slew and loadingDetours and new fanout consume margin; hold can also change
Analog reviewA new switching route can couple into references or alter a return path
Checkpoint: can a metal-only ECO use a normal filler as a NAND?

No. A standard filler does not contain the NAND transistors. Use suitable preplaced spare devices, or change the mask scope. The macro’s hidden gates also need a verified abstraction/netlist for the modified implementation.

Check your understanding — why spread spare cells across the core instead of grouping them in one corner?

A metal-only change must wire a spare gate to the logic it fixes. A distant spare needs long new routes that add delay, coupling and congestion, and they may not fit in the layers allowed to change. Spares spread evenly keep every region within reach.

In the open SKY130 flow. LibreLane documents ECO steps that insert buffers or diodes into an existing layout between flow steps; larger changes are scripted against OpenROAD’s database. Commercial flows use the ECO features of Innovus or IC Compiler II, together with tools such as Conformal ECO. Whatever makes the edit, rerun extraction, timing, DRC and LVS afterward.

Key takeaways

  • A spare cell is gates placed early so a metal-only change can reconnect them.
  • macro_sparecell is 29 sites, 13.34 × 2.72 µm, leakage 0.04487112 nW.
  • Inside: two nand2_2, two nor2_2, two inv_2, one conb_1.
  • The only signal pin is LO, function 0, max load 1.8948 pF, gate antenna 1.980 µm².
  • Ordinary fill cells do not provide spare logic gates; spare cells deliberately contain reusable logic.
  • A metal-only ECO may reconnect existing spare logic, within the allowed mask set; every ECO still needs connectivity, physical, and timing verification.
  • Pre-freeze ECO cell inserts (Odb.InsertECOBuffers, Odb.InsertECODiodes) are lesson 57; they are not a metal-only change.
