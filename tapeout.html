Tapeout, MPW & Tiny Tapeout

What you actually hand to a foundry shuttle: the views in LibreLane’s final/ directory (the Lab 6 SPM is the recorded example), a signoff checklist, how Caravel and a multi-project wafer fit together, and Tiny Tapeout’s published SKY130 tile and pinout — including the two official size figures, which are not quite the same number.

Where the facts come from. Deliverable files and sizes are the Lab 6 SPM run RUN_2026-11-11_16-09-06, already listed with byte counts in lesson 8’s tour of final/ and with DRC, XOR and LVS results in Lab 6 and lesson 62. This lesson does not re-run that flow, and it does not run Efabless precheck or a Tiny Tapeout GitHub Action. Tiny Tapeout sizes and pins are from official pages fetched on 11 November 2026: the FAQ “Chip Specs for TT04 to TT10” (“about 160×100 µm”, Skywater 130 nm, clock and nreset, 8 inputs, 8 outputs, 8 bidirectional IOs, a claimed ≥50 MHz, 6–9 months to manufacture) and the “Creating Silicon Art” guide (SKY 1×1 = 161 × 112 µm, 2×2 = 335 × 226 µm; IHP 1×1 = 202 × 155 µm), which tells you to check tile_sizes.yaml for the exact number on your shuttle. The raw tile_sizes.yaml at the previously published tt-support-tools path returned HTTP 404 on that day, so this lesson does not invent a third “exact” figure. A 167 × 108 µm figure that appears in some third-party templates is not used. Verilog port names (ui_in, uo_out, uio_*, clk, rst_n, ena) are the published Tiny Tapeout HDL template convention matching those pin counts. Multi-project wafers in general are lesson 2.

What you will learn

  • Name the views in a LibreLane final/ directory and point at the recorded SPM sizes.
  • Walk a signoff checklist that covers lint, multi-corner STA, DRC, LVS, XOR, antenna and density, without claiming a precheck run this course did not perform.
  • Place Caravel, an MPW and Tiny Tapeout relative to each other: same process family, different sockets and different die area.
  • Quote Tiny Tapeout’s official SKY130 tile sizes as published, and know that the shuttle’s tile_sizes.yaml is the authority for a given run.

Why it matters
A clean GDS on your disk is necessary and not sufficient. The shuttle has a socket, a calendar, a legal form, and a checklist that includes things (manifest, wrapper pin match, licence) that DRC will not catch.

3. MPW and Caravel

A multi-project wafer (MPW) is a shared mask set: many designs, one lot, a fraction of the cost of a dedicated run. Lesson 2 is the process and the economics. Caravel is one socket used on SKY130 MPWs run around the Efabless / Google open-shuttle programme: a common pad ring and management SoC, and your design in the 2920 × 3520 µm hole. You tape out a wrapper GDS that matches that socket; the shuttle integrator stitches wrappers into a reticle.

The practical difference from Lab 6 is the top of the hierarchy. Lab 6’s top is spm, with flow-assigned pins. A Caravel tapeout’s top is user_project_wrapper, with the fixed 637-signal-pin DEF of lesson 56. Signoff is then on the wrapper, or on a macro plus a wrapper, according to the hardening strategy you chose — and on the integrator’s precheck, which this course has not run.

4. Tiny Tapeout

Tiny Tapeout is a tighter socket on the same idea: many very small designs share a frame, and each designer gets a tile (or a multiple of a tile) rather than a full Caravel user area. It is a different contract — fewer pins, much less area — not a different process family. Official pages, fetched on 11 November 2026, are the source for every figure in this section; none of them is a measurement we made on a GDS.

Tile size, two official figures. The FAQ, under “Chip Specs for TT04 to TT10,” describes a standard tile as “about 160×100 µm,” about 1,000 gates, Skywater 130 nm. The “Creating Silicon Art” guide on the same site is more precise and tells you to check tile_sizes.yaml: as a guide, a SKY 1×1 tile is 161 × 112 µm and a 2×2 is 335 × 226 µm; an IHP 1×1 is 202 × 155 µm and a 2×2 is 420 × 314 µm. This course is SKY130, so the SKY numbers are the relevant guide. The yaml itself was not retrievable at the previously published GitHub path on the day of writing (HTTP 404), so there is no third “exact” number here. If you submit, read the yaml for your shuttle. A 167 × 108 µm figure that appears in some third-party comments is not cited as official and is not used.

Pins, from the FAQ, and the HDL names that go with them. The FAQ lists a clock, an nreset (low to reset), 8 inputs, 8 outputs and 8 bidirectional IOs. The published Tiny Tapeout Verilog template names those ports clk, rst_n, ui_in[7:0], uo_out[7:0], uio_in / uio_out / uio_oe, and an ena that is high when the tile is selected. The FAQ also states a ≥50 MHz claim and a 6–9 month manufacture time; those are the programme’s published figures, not a frequency this course measured and not a calendar we can guarantee.

A Tiny Tapeout submission is typically a GitHub repository: a Verilog top that matches those ports, an info.yaml with the project’s metadata, and a GitHub Action that the programme provides — including a gate-level test. This course has not run that Action and does not attach a log. The HDL still has to survive the patterns of lesson 33 and the signoff of section 2; the tile is just a smaller socket. A 2×2 SKY tile (335 × 226 µm, per the same guide) is the next step up when a 1×1 will not fit, at the cost of more tiles on the shuttle.

IHP figures are included only so that a student who switches shuttles does not apply 161 × 112 µm to the wrong process. They are not the default for this SKY130 course.

The FAQ says a TT tile is “about 160×100 µm,” and the Silicon Art guide says 161 × 112 µm for SKY 1×1. Which number should a student use?

For a submission, the shuttle’s own tile_sizes.yaml. For this lesson, both official figures are quoted, and 161 × 112 is the more precise SKY guide. This course does not pick a third number, and it does not use 167 × 108. The 404 on the previously published yaml path is why the yaml itself is not tabulated here.

Can you tick “IR drop clean” on the SPM because Lab 6 finished 80/80 steps?

No. On that run IR reporting was configured and was not executed (the signoff resume began at Magic.StreamOut). Lessons 62 and 63 already record that. A completed flow is not a completed IR report.

What is the difference between Lab 6’s top and a Caravel tapeout’s top?

Lab 6’s top is spm, with pins the flow assigned. A Caravel tapeout’s top is user_project_wrapper, with a fixed DEF: 2920 × 3520 µm, 637 signal pins and 8 supplies. Signoff then applies to that wrapper (or to a macro plus wrapper), and the shuttle’s precheck is additional work this course has not run.
