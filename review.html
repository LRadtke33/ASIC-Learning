The original 34 lessons retain their reviewed corrections, the first eight bridge lessons remain in place, and this edition adds eight deeper lessons plus five experiments. The course now supports prediction, execution, measurement, and explanation across analog, digital, and physical design.

1. What the evidence means

The first 42-lesson revision was a source, topology, and analytical review. This edition adds actual SPICE, digital simulation, logic synthesis, functional-equivalence, and numerical-analysis results. The original analytical examples remain analytical; the new lab evidence does not retroactively validate every diagram or assumption as a physical implementation.

6. Current state and worthwhile next work

This is now a substantial introductory-to-intermediate cross-training course with a practical laboratory layer. Its strongest feature is the connection between a digital abstraction, an analog measurement, and a physical cause. Readers can reproduce important mechanisms and see where a plausible hand estimate stops explaining the result.

The next technical step is a project-owned flow: real device layouts, approved DRC/LVS/PEX, schematic-versus-extracted correlation, constrained STA with real interfaces, and noise/statistical characterization with supported models. A transistor-level reference with a physical startup circuit and a measured feedback-loop return ratio would deepen the analog sequence. A realistic ADC front end with driver, reference and extraction would connect Labs 2–4.

The next teaching step is a pilot with one analog-focused and one digital-focused learner. Observe where they need prerequisites, how long the labs take, and whether the checks reveal the intended misconception. This edition has technical review and reproducible execution; it does not claim a classroom learning-outcome study.
