Caravel Integration: The User Project Wrapper

The Efabless Caravel harness gives a user project a fixed pin list, four supply domains, 38 GPIOs and a 2920 × 3520 µm area. This lesson walks the actual user_project_wrapper.v shipped with LibreLane 3.0.17, and the three official hardening strategies in LibreLane’s Caravel documentation.

Where the facts come from. The port list, power-pin comments, Wishbone, logic-analyzer, GPIO, analog, clock and interrupt declarations are from the wrapper Verilog at librelane/examples/spm-user_project_wrapper/user_project_wrapper.v (Apache-2.0, Efabless), with defines.v in the same directory: MPRJ_IO_PADS_1 = 19, MPRJ_IO_PADS_2 = 19, MPRJ_IO_PADS = 38. Copies are in labs/analysis_examples/caravel/. Die, padframe and user-area sizes (3.60 × 5.20 mm die, 3.588 × 5.188 mm padframe, 2920 × 3520 µm user area, 637 signal pins plus 8 supplies, QFN-64 9 × 9 mm) are the course measurements already used in lesson 1, lesson 5 and lesson 55 — they are not re-measured here. The three hardening strategies are from the LibreLane 3.0.17 documentation under docs/source/additional_material/caravel/ (macro-first, flattened wrapper, top-level integration). This course has not hardened a full Caravel wrapper; Lab 6 hardened the SPM as a standalone design. Housekeeping-SPI and management-firmware behaviour is described from that documentation and from the course’s earlier Caravel lessons, not from a firmware trace we ran.

What you will learn

  • Name every port class on user_project_wrapper from the file itself: Wishbone, 128-bit logic analyzer, 38 GPIOs, analog I/O, user_clock2, three IRQs, and eight supply pins.
  • Place the 2920 × 3520 µm user area on the Caravel die, and know that 637 signal pins on the wrapper perimeter are a floorplan constraint, not a suggestion.
  • Choose among LibreLane’s three documented ways to harden a project into that wrapper, and know which example settings go with each.
  • Leave the management SoC and housekeeping SPI in the harness’s job, not in the user RTL, unless you have a reason to touch them.

Why it matters
A SKY130 project that is going to an Efabless-style MPW does not pick its own pad ring. It fills a socket whose pins, supplies and area are already decided. Mis-naming a pin or omitting a power connection fails integration, not just DRC.

1. The socket, not a blank die

Caravel is a harness: a management SoC, a pad ring, a housekeeping SPI, and a rectangular hole in the middle for you. Your top module is not the chip; it is user_project_wrapper, and the pin list of that module is the contract with the harness. The wrapper Verilog shipped with LibreLane says so at the top of the file: it “enumerates all of the pins available to the user,” and the bundled SPM_example “should be removed and replaced with the actual user project.”

The physical hole is 2920 × 3520 µm, from the wrapper DEF already quoted in lesson 55. The die around it is 3.60 × 5.20 mm, the padframe 3.588 × 5.188 mm, the package a 9 × 9 mm QFN-64 — those figures are lesson 1 and lesson 5. LibreLane’s Caravel documentation sometimes approximates the user area as “≈ 3 mm × 3.6 mm”; this course keeps the DEF number. The wrapper perimeter carries 637 signal pins and 8 supply pins. Lesson 55 already computed the average signal-pin pitch: the perimeter is 2 × (2920 + 3520) = 12,880 µm, about 20 µm between signal pins.

2. Every port, from the Verilog

GPIO width comes from defines.v: 19 pads on each of two sides, 38 in total, matching the “38 user GPIOs” already used in the anatomy lessons. The analog bus is declared as inout [`MPRJ_IO_PADS-10:0] analog_io — 29 bits, indices [28:0]. The comment in the file is precise and is worth taking literally: “analog I/O is not available on the 7 lowest-numbered GPIO pads, and so the analog_io indexing is offset from the GPIO indexing by 7 (also upper 2 GPIOs do not have analog_io).” So GPIO[0]–GPIO[6] and the two highest GPIOs have no analog_io companion; analog_io[0] sits on GPIO[7].

The eight supplies are wrapped in `ifdef USE_POWER_PINS. Comments in the file: vccd1 / vccd2 are “User area 1/2 1.8V supply”; vdda1 / vdda2 are “3.3V supply”; vssa* analog ground, vssd* digital ground. A digital-only project typically hangs on vccd1 / vssd1, as the bundled SPM_example does. Mixed-signal macros that want the 3.3 V analog rails must connect vdda / vssa and respect the domain crossing — lesson 54 and the floorplan’s macro power-ring notes.

defines.v also records GPIO power-on defaults: “Most I/Os set to be user input pins on startup,” with MGMT_INIT 1'b0, OENB_INIT 1'b0, DM_INIT 3'b001, and a note that GPIOs 5 to 35 are “being set from a build-time-programmable block.” Those are the file’s words, not a measurement of a powered Caravel on a bench.

3. Wishbone, as the wrapper presents it

The wrapper is a Wishbone slave (the comment says “WB MI A”). The management SoC is the master: it drives wb_clk_i, wb_rst_i, strobe, cycle, write-enable, byte select, address and write data; the user project drives acknowledge and read data. A classic-cycle picture is below. It is a teaching waveform of the protocol, not a simulation of the bundled SPM_example.

The 128-bit logic-analyzer bus (la_data_in, la_data_out, la_oenb) is a debug hook from the management SoC: the harness can observe or override user signals without using GPIOs. user_irq[2:0] is how the user project raises maskable interrupts to the management processor. user_clock2 is a second clock, “on independent integer divider” in the file’s comment — useful when the Wishbone clock is not the clock you want for the datapath. Crossing between wb_clk_i and user_clock2 is a CDC problem (lesson 39), not a free connection.

4. Three official ways to harden the wrapper

LibreLane’s Caravel documentation describes three strategies for turning user RTL into a wrapper GDS. They are documented recipes, not runs this course has reproduced. The names and the settings below are from those pages.

StrategyIdeaSettings the docs highlightWhen the docs point to it
Macro-firstHarden the user design (the docs’ example is AES + a Wishbone shim) as a macro, then place that macro inside a wrapper that is mostly routing and power.On the inner macro: RT_MAX_LAYER = met4, PDN_MULTILAYER = false, so the wrapper can still use met5 for its own PDN and route over the macro.A large, already-closed block that you do not want the wrapper flow to flatten or re-place.

No macros.SYNTH_ELABORATE_ONLY = false; CTS, fill, well taps and IR-drop reporting on.A modest digital project that can be treated as a single flat netlist, similar in spirit to Lab 6’s standalone SPM, but with the wrapper pin list as the top.
Top-level integrationThe user design is a macro, but the wrapper flow is allowed to run its usual optimizations around it.The docs’ AES example sets RUN_IRDROP_REPORT = false. That is the example’s choice, not a recommendation to skip IR in general — and Lab 6’s original folder recorded that IR was configured but not executed; a later OpenROAD analysis of that final/ is in lesson 63.A middle path: a hardened macro, with the wrapper still doing CTS, fill and routing at the top.

Two cautions follow from those pages and from this course’s own runs. First, a pin-order file and the wrapper DEF fix the pin positions; a floorplan that ignores them will not drop into the harness. Second, RUN_IRDROP_REPORT being true is not the same as an IR report existing — the SPM run in Lab 6 is the worked example of that distinction (lesson 38, lesson 63). If you adopt the top-level-integration example’s false, you are choosing to skip the report, and you should say so.

5. What stays in the harness

Caravel’s management SoC and housekeeping SPI sit outside the user area. The management processor can read and write the Wishbone slave, drive the logic-analyzer bus, and (through housekeeping) observe or set GPIO modes. Firmware that runs on that processor is part of bringing a Caravel chip up, but this course has not run that firmware and does not include a trace of it. For a first user project, the practical rule is: implement the wrapper contract, connect the supplies you use, and leave the pad ring, the SPI and the management SoC alone.

OpenRAM or DFFRAM macros inside the user area still take vccd1 / vssd1 (or the domain you assigned) and a power ring — lesson 43 and the macro-ring section of lesson 55. They do not get a private pad.

How many user GPIO pads does the wrapper have, and where is that number declared?

38. defines.v sets MPRJ_IO_PADS_1 and MPRJ_IO_PADS_2 to 19 each, and MPRJ_IO_PADS to their sum. The wrapper ports are io_in, io_out and io_oeb, each [`MPRJ_IO_PADS-1:0]. That matches the 38 user GPIOs already used in lesson 5.

Why is analog_io 29 bits wide, not 38?

The declaration is [`MPRJ_IO_PADS-10:0], and the file’s comment explains it: the 7 lowest-numbered GPIOs have no analog connection, the upper 2 also have none, and the analog index is offset by 7. 38 − 7 − 2 = 29.

If you harden a large AES block first and then drop it into the wrapper, which documented strategy is that, and what happens to met5?

Macro-first. The inner macro is documented with RT_MAX_LAYER = met4 and PDN_MULTILAYER = false, so met5 remains available for the wrapper’s power grid and for routes that cross the macro. This course has not re-run that flow; the settings are from LibreLane’s documentation.
