Lesson 57 / 84 · Part VI, Physical cells and the floorplan

Hierarchical Design, Abstracts & ECO

A hardened block is handed up as three abstracts — a LEF for the shape, a Liberty for the timing, a Verilog for the function — and a late change is an ECO, either a pre-freeze cell insert or a metal-only reconnect of spare gates.

Where the facts come from. LibreLane’s documented ECO steps are quoted from the Classic 3.0.17 guide docs/source/usage/using_ecos.md: Odb.InsertECOBuffers and Odb.InsertECODiodes are not in the default flow; they are injected with meta.substituting_steps, and a run can resume with --with-initial-state pointing at a previous state_out.json and --from the ECO step. The guide’s demonstrative netlist (dfrtp_4 clocked cleanly, dfrtp_1 clocked through a clkbuf_4, RUN_POST_*_RESIZER_TIMING false) is that document’s example; this course has not run it. Hierarchical abstracts and timing budgets are teaching, grounded in how lesson 56 describes a user macro inside the Caravel wrapper. This course has not hardened a full wrapper, and Lab 6’s SPM is a standalone block. Metal-only ECO and macro_sparecell remain in lesson 53 — this lesson complements that one, it does not replace it.

What you will learn

  • Name the three abstracts a hardened macro presents to a top-level flow, and what each is for.
  • Say why a timing budget exists, and what goes wrong if a child is late against a budget the parent already closed.
  • Distinguish a LibreLane pre-freeze ECO insert (Odb.InsertECOBuffers / InsertECODiodes, --with-initial-state) from a metal-only spare-cell ECO.

1. A hardened child is an abstract at the parent

Once a block is placed, routed and signed off, the parent should not see every standard cell inside it. It sees:

  • A LEF abstract — the outline, the pins, the blockage that says “do not route through this interior,” and the power pins the parent’s grid must connect. LibreLane’s Magic step for writing a LEF of a finished layout is Magic.WriteLEF; the parent’s floorplan then places that macro like any other LEF cell. This course has not emitted a LEF of the Lab 6 SPM for a parent chip.
  • A Liberty — setup, hold, delay and power at the block’s ports, at the corners the parent will time. It can be characterized (the idea of lesson 24, at block scale) or written as a budget the child is contracted to meet.
  • A Verilog (or formal) model — enough function for the parent’s simulation and equivalence, without the child’s gates.

Caravel’s three hardening strategies (lesson 56) are this idea at chip scale: a user project can be a finished macro sitting in the wrapper, or the wrapper can be flattened around it. Lab 6 hardened the SPM alone; it is a child with no parent in this course.

2. Timing budgets

A path that starts in one block and ends in another has to have its slack split: so many picoseconds for the child’s output delay, so many for the parent route, so many for the receiving child’s setup. That split is a budget. If the child later comes in 40 ps late against its Liberty, the parent that already closed is now open — or the parent’s margin was fictional all along. The honest order is: agree the budget, close the child against it, re-time the parent with the real abstract, and only then sign off the chip.

Hierarchical STA can also miss interface crossings that a flattened STA would see. A conservative budget and a final flattened (or extracted-interface) check are the usual insurance. This course has not run a hierarchical STA; Lab 6’s timing is a flat block at the SPM.

Check your understanding — the child’s Liberty says output delay 200 ps; a later characterisation measures 240 ps. The parent still meets setup. Are you done?

Not until the parent is re-timed with the new Liberty. Meeting setup against the old 200 ps abstract is not a signoff of the 240 ps block. If the parent still closes, you update the abstract and record that; if it does not, the budget was too tight and someone has to move.

3. Two kinds of ECO

Lesson 53 is the metal-only case: base layers frozen, spare gates already sitting in the layout, a change made by reconnecting them. LibreLane also documents a pre-freeze ECO, while cells may still be added:

  • Odb.InsertECOBuffers inserts a named buffer on a driver or a sink, typically to repair a hold (or to add delay on a short path).
  • Odb.InsertECODiodes inserts a diode on a sink, for an antenna fix. The guide notes that the diode step supports sinks, not drivers.

Neither is in the default Classic flow. The documented way to inject them is meta.substituting_steps, for example placing Odb.InsertECOBuffers immediately before a (re)run of OpenROAD.DetailedRouting. To start from an already-routed database instead of from RTL, the guide uses:

librelane --with-initial-state …/state_out.json --from odb.insertecobuffers ./config.yaml

The guide’s worked netlist delays u_ff2’s clock through a sky130_fd_sc_hd__clkbuf_4 and turns post-CTS and post-GRT resizer timing off, so a hold fail is guaranteed; it then inserts sky130_fd_sc_hd__buf_1 on u_ff1/Q. That is the document’s demonstration. This course has not reproduced it, and a real hold repair still has to be checked at every corner — the same guide notes that one buf_1 cleared typical and left a fail at fast, which a second buffer then addressed. The incremental detailed-route step is documented as routing only the nets the ECO touched.

A buffer inserted this way is a new cell: it needs a site, and it is not a metal-only change. After tapeout, or after a foundry freeze of the base layers, you are back in lesson 53’s world of macro_sparecell.

Check your understanding — you found a hold fail after detailed route. Is Odb.InsertECOBuffers a metal-only ECO?

No. It places a new buffer (a cell with transistors) and then incrementally re-routes the touched nets. That is legal only while base layers may still change. A metal-only ECO has to use gates that are already in the GDS, which is why spare cells are placed in advance (lesson 53).

Check your understanding — why does the official example turn the resizer off?

Because the point is to demonstrate a manual ECO on a known hold, not to test whether the automatic resizer would have fixed it. The guide says so explicitly: in a realistic run the resizer will usually fix something this simple. Do not take RUN_POST_*_RESIZER_TIMING: false as advice for a production block.

Key takeaways

  • A hardened child is handed up as LEF, Liberty and a functional model. The Liberty may be a budget; the child still has to meet it, and the parent has to be re-timed against the real abstract.
  • LibreLane’s Odb.InsertECOBuffers and Odb.InsertECODiodes are documented pre-freeze inserts, injected into Classic, resumable with --with-initial-state. This course has not run that flow.
  • Metal-only ECO is lesson 53: spare cells already placed. A newly inserted buffer is not metal-only.
