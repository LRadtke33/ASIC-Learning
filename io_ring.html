Lesson 64 / 84 · Part VII, The chip boundary

The I/O Ring

The core is rows of 0.46 µm sites. The outside of the chip is a ring of pad cells, much taller, abutted so their supply metal meets.

Where the numbers come from. Outlines and pin shapes come from the sky130_fd_io LEF files. The abutment rule is the I/O user guide. Dimensions below use the pinned upstream I/O LEFs; installed wrapper cells can differ.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish pad cells from standard cells, and read their SKY130 sizes.
  • Explain how abutted pad cells form the ring’s supply buses on met4 and met5.
  • Apply the power–ground pad pairing rule and keep the ring’s several supplies separate.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
Chip-level work in step 4, floorplan and power grid.
Tools
Caravel supplies a finished pad ring; LibreLane’s Chip flow builds one with OpenROAD’s pad-ring tool. Commercial: I/O planning in Innovus or IC Compiler II.
Why it matters
The pad ring is the chip’s only connection to the package and its first defense against static discharge.

1. Pads are not standard cells

A standard cell is 2.72 µm tall and snaps to a 0.46 µm site (lesson 13). A pad cell is the circuit that faces the package. In this I/O library the general-purpose pad top_gpiov2 is class PAD, 80 µm wide by 200 µm tall, with symmetry X Y R90 so the same outline can sit on any side of the chip. It has 39 pins. The LEF electrical access pin PAD is on met5. A LEF pin rectangle is not by itself the physical passivation opening or an approved bond target. That plate is lesson 67.

The user guide says the library's I/O supply range is 1.71 V to 5.5 V. It describes gpiov2 as the buffer whose receiver can take 1.8 V signaling when the I/O supply is not 1.8 V. The over-voltage cell top_gpio_ovtv2 is a different outline, 140 × 200 µm.

2. The ring is the buses that meet

The user guide says every power and ground pad has the I/O bussing running through it on met5 and met4, and that abutting those pads forms the ring. The same sentence is repeated for the reset cells. The reset cell in the public list is top_xres4v2, 75 × 200 µm.

On top_gpiov2 the supply pins are not stripes across the cell. VDDIO is met4, 1.27 µm wide, on both side edges: one piece 24.965 µm tall (y 70.035 to 95.000) and a shorter piece 4.65 µm tall. VSSIO's tall piece is 1.27 × 24.215 µm at the top edge, y 175.785 to 200. The other rails (VCCD, VSSD, VDDA, VSSA, and the quiet and hibernate rails) are the same kind of edge pair. When two cells abut, those edge shapes touch. That is the connection.

The guide also says one side of a power or ground pad is DRC-clean against a gpio or an sio macro, and the other side is not. The restriction it states is to pair a power pad with a ground pad. Do not drop a single power pad between two signal pads and assume both sides are legal.

The corner cell's pins are only the ring supplies and AMUXBUS_A / AMUXBUS_B, on met4 and met5. VDDIO on that cell is a 1.27 × 24.960 µm met4 piece on the side and a 24.960 × 1.27 µm piece on the bottom, with a met5 piece 24.950 µm the same way. The corner turns the bus. The LEF height is 203.665 µm.

Obstruction metal is a different shape from the pin. Inside top_gpiov2 the OBS includes an 80.0 × 53.835 µm met4 rectangle. A 76.66 × 25.765 µm met4 obstruction covers the same y range as the tall VDDIO pin, but it starts at x 1.67 while the pin ends at x 1.27. There is a 0.40 µm gap. A gap like this is not evidence of a short, and the 24.965 µm pin is not the height of the bus through the whole cell; only the full layout shows those details.

The upstream sky130_fd_io subset should not be confused with the complete installed I/O kit: sky130_ef_io extensions supply common-bus slice fillers. Their placement and domain rules must match the chosen wrapper library. How a gap is closed is lesson 66. The clamps inside the power and ground cells are lesson 65. The ohms along the met4 stub are lesson 68.

3. What a ring looks like around a chip

Figure 1 is four cells touching, with the measured outlines labeled on them. A chip puts that abutment on all four sides. The pads face the package. The corner cell turns the met4 and met5 buses. Inside the ring, the standard-cell rows of lesson 13 fill the core. The core rails of lesson 14 eventually tie to the pad supplies. That tie, and the ohms along one met4 stub, are lesson 68.

The drawing below is the usual pad-ring picture: signal pads, a power pad, a ground pad, four corner cells, a bus that continues where the cells abut, and rows in the middle. A complete kit may also include sky130_ef_io fillers, disconnects, and wrappers; choose a compatible ring architecture rather than assuming every public master can abut every other master. In the open SKY130 flow. On a Caravel project the pad ring is already built. For a custom chip, OpenROAD’s pad-ring tool places the pad cells, corners and fillers from a description of each side of the die, and LibreLane’s Chip flow runs it as its PadRing step. Commercial place-and-route tools have equivalent I/O planning commands.

4. Power and ground face each other

The user guide's placement rule for these pads is a pair. One side of a power or ground pad is not DRC-clean against a signal pad, so that side faces the other half of the pair. Signal pads sit on the clean side.

A single power pad between two signal pads puts the unclean side against a signal pad. The pair, power then ground or ground then power, is the arrangement the guide states. Section 2 has the bus metals that pair is there to continue.

5. The ring has several supplies, not one VDD

In particular, VDDIO is not automatically the 1.8 V core supply. Connecting differently named rails requires the intended circuit or approved strap/disconnect cell, not a naming shortcut. Verify powered, unpowered, reset, and test configurations. [I/O modes and supply connections]

Worked example: eight 80 µm GPIO cells plus one 75 µm power pad and one 75 µm ground pad occupy 8 × 80 + 2 × 75 = 790 µm along a straight side before corners, fillers, spacing exceptions, and wrappers. This estimates perimeter demand, not die size.

Analog ↔ digital: a digital pad configuration can connect or disconnect an analog bus. Its logic truth table, supply state, resistor path, and physical rail continuity must be reviewed together.

Check your understanding — Does an OBS rectangle prove that two supply pins are connected through it?

No. A LEF obstruction restricts routing. Internal connectivity comes from the IP design/netlist and detailed layout, and resistance requires a suitable extracted or characterized model.

Check your understanding — why can VDDIO not be joined to the 1.8 V core supply just because both are power?

VDDIO is the I/O supply and may be 3.3 V. Joining it to VCCD would put that voltage across 1.8 V thin-oxide devices, or force the I/O cells away from their design point. Different rails connect only through intended circuits or approved strap and disconnect cells.

Key takeaways

  • top_gpiov2 is 80 × 200 µm, class PAD, 39 pins. The core row is 2.72 µm.
  • Power and ground pads are 75 µm wide. HV outlines are 200 µm tall. LV outlines are 198 µm tall.
  • The ring is met4 and met5 that meet when the cells abut. The corner cell turns those buses and is 200 × 203.665 µm.
  • On the gpio, the supply pins are 1.27 µm edge stubs, not a stripe across the cell.
  • Pair a power pad with a ground pad. The guide says one side of those pads is not DRC-clean against a signal pad.
