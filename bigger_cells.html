Anatomy of a Bigger Cell

A NAND2 in polygons: shared diffusion, shared contacts, M1 wiring — and how routing-grid alignment helps the router reach a cell.

What you will learn

  • Read series and parallel transistors in silicon: shared diffusion, shared contacts and gate strips.
  • Follow a NAND2 from schematic to polygons, including its uncontacted series node.
  • Explain how preferred tracks and pin access decide whether the router can reach a cell.

1. Four transistors, three new tricks

Lesson 16 used an inverter to introduce transistor geometry. A NAND2 adds series and parallel networks. This lesson illustrates diffusion sharing and internal interconnect, then explains why those choices affect routing above the cell. The colored polygons are conceptual: actual SKY130 cells use diffusion, poly, local interconnect, contacts and metal according to the verified cell views, not necessarily the cartoon’s exact layers or order.

2. Series and parallel, in silicon

Series devices (the NMOS stack): two gates can cross one continuous diffusion strip. The uncontacted region between them is the internal node n1, so no metal connection is required at that particular junction. This saves contacts and space, but the shared diffusion still has resistance and junction capacitance. “Uncontacted shared diffusion” is a useful clue to a series connection, not proof that the node is electrically ideal.

Parallel (the PMOS pair): both devices connect VDD ↔ ZN, so lay them on one strip as source–gate–drain–gate–source: the middle drain contact is shared by both transistors. Two devices, three contact columns instead of four — again smaller. (When neighboring devices must not connect, the strip gets a diffusion break — or in continuous-diffusion processes, a dummy gate tied off to act as the isolation.)

3. The full NAND2

In the teaching cell, M1 supply straps and an output strap connect the diffusion regions. Where M1 crosses poly without a contact stack, the conductors are not galvanically connected; they can still couple capacitively. Such a crossing must meet the applicable layer, spacing and enclosure rules. The simplified picture does not establish DRC compliance.

The cell in Figure 2 is drawn four sites wide so each contact has room. sky130_fd_sc_hd__nand2_1 is three sites, the same outline as inv_1. Section 6 is that netlist and that LEF.

4. Connecting to it: preferred tracks and legal access

The teaching cell exposes M1 pins, so a convenient access route comes from vertical M2 through a via. Routing on preferred tracks improves predictable access and capacity. Real flows may use M1 routes, legal off-track access and wrong-way jogs; SKY130 HD signal pins in section 6 are on li1. A routing-grid departure is not itself a fabrication violation: manufacturing-grid, width, spacing, enclosure and all other DRC rules still determine legality.

5. When the cell itself needs M2

An internal M2 jumper can cross an M1 net through two vias. Aligning it with preferred tracks can reduce its cost to external routing, but its blockage includes via enclosures and spacing halos, and the exact capacity loss depends on geometry. The library abstract must describe the relevant obstruction; “exactly one track” is only the simplified drawing’s result.

6. The library NAND2 is three sites, with the series node in silicon

Figure 2 is drawn four sites wide so the contacts have room to see. sky130_fd_sc_hd__nand2_1 is SIZE 1.380 BY 2.720, three sites, the same outline as inv_1. The SPICE netlist is the lesson 10 schematic with this library's transistor:

  • Two high-Vt PMOS, each 1.0 µm wide and 0.15 µm long, in parallel from VPWR to Y. Gates are A and B.
  • Two NMOS, each W = 0.65 µm and L = 0.15 µm, form the series pull-down. B is next to VGND and A next to Y. The internal series node is present in SPICE but is not exposed as a LEF signal pin; that does not establish its physical conductor layers.

The Liberty function is (!A) | (!B), a NAND. Pin caps at the typical corner are 2.315 fF on A and 2.324 fF on B, the same size as inv_1's 2.302 fF input, because each input sees one NMOS and one PMOS of those same widths. max_capacitance on Y is 0.1666 pF. The cell LEF has no OBS block.

The LEF omits the internal series node as a signal pin. That does not by itself prove there is no metal on that node: an abstract is not a complete internal layout. The contact-free diffusion sharing in section 2 is a valid compact implementation, but proving the exact internal geometry of nand2_1 requires its GDS/extracted connectivity, not the absence of an OBS shape.

A is the li1 rectangle from (0.94, 1.075) to (1.275, 1.325). B is from (0.095, 1.055) to (0.430, 1.325). Both sit in the middle band of the 2.72 µm row, on local interconnect, the same layer as inv_1's pins. The met1 shapes in this cell are the two rails, 0.48 µm tall, the same rectangles as the inverter. Figure 3's story, that the router arrives from met2, still applies. It arrives onto li1 through met1 and mcon, one layer lower than the teaching drawing.

Rise and fall caps differ slightly, and they differ between the pins. A is 2.375 fF rising and 2.254 fF falling. B is 2.428 fF rising and 2.220 fF falling. The single capacitance number is the average a fanout count uses. Area in Liberty is 3.7536 µm², the same three sites as inv_1.

7. An Euler path is why the series node has no contact

An Euler trail visits each transistor edge once in the connectivity graph. A common gate order in the NMOS and PMOS graphs can reduce diffusion breaks. In the verified SKY130 NAND2 netlist, the walk from VGND goes through B then A to Y. Figure 5 shows that ordering. Swapping the Boolean inputs preserves NAND functionality, although characterized timing arcs may differ.

The parallel PMOS network can also use one continuous strip: VPWR → gate B → Y → gate A → VPWR. The two outer source regions are wired together to VPWR and the shared middle drain goes to Y. Thus a common B,A gate order is possible on both strips. A contact-free shared internal region is not the only possible layout, and shared diffusion by itself does not distinguish series from parallel devices.

Worked example — an Euler trail, and what a break costs

Section 7 already walks the HD nand2_1: the NMOS trail from VGND is B then A to Y, and a matching PMOS order lets the series node sit in the diffusion without a contact. Here is the counting exercise on that same graph.

The connectivity graph of a 2-input NAND has four transistor edges (two NMOS, two PMOS) and three node vertices on each side (VSS, the series node, Y on the NMOS side; VDD, the series node, Y on the PMOS side). An Euler trail exists on each side if at most two vertices have odd degree. With the shared gate order (B, A):

  • Each side is a single trail: VSS —B— n1 —A— Y, and VDD —B— p1 —A— Y. There is one diffusion strip per side, and the internal series node has no contact (Figure 5).
  • If the NMOS gates are ordered (B, A) but the PMOS gates are ordered (A, B), each side still has an Euler trail of its own, but the two trails no longer share a gate order. The poly must then be drawn in a different left-to-right sequence on the two sides, or a diffusion break is introduced so that a new strip can start. The HD cell did not do this: it kept a common order and a continuous strip.
  • A diffusion break is a site (or a fraction of one) that no longer holds a transistor — it is empty diffusion or a shallow-trench gap, paid in width. On a three-site NAND2 (1.38 µm), a one-site break would be a large fraction of the cell; that is why the Euler condition is worth checking before the polygons are drawn.

The exercise is to write the two trails and to say whether a proposed gate swap still has a common order. The HD nand2_1 is the worked answer: common order (B, A), no break, series node uncontacted. Swapping Boolean pin names without swapping the walk does not change the transistor count, but it does change which input is closer to the rail, which can matter for delay — a different question, taken up in lesson 11.

Worked checkpoint — Does a continuous diffusion strip prove series operation?

No. Trace the named nodes. The NMOS walk VGND → B → n1 → A → Y places both devices in the pull-down path. The PMOS walk VPWR → B → Y → A → VPWR has both outer regions tied to VPWR, so either device connects VPWR to Y: they are parallel. The same B,A gate order can therefore support continuous strips in both networks. Contacts and net connections determine the topology.

Check your understanding — why does the node between the two series NMOS of a NAND2 need no contact?

Nothing outside the stack connects to it. The two transistors share the diffusion between their gates, which makes the series connection directly in silicon; a contact would only add area and capacitance. An Euler path through the transistor graph finds the gate order that allows this sharing.

Key takeaways

  • An uncontacted shared diffusion region can implement a series node, as drawn here. A netlist or missing LEF pin does not prove which conductor layers implement that node.
  • A continuous strip can also implement parallel devices when contacts join the appropriate regions; trace both outer and middle nodes.
  • An M1/poly crossing without a contact stack is not a galvanic connection, but it can add parasitic capacitance.
  • The static CMOS NAND2 shown uses two NMOS and two PMOS. Layout fingers, site count and logical device count are different quantities.
  • Preferred-track alignment can improve access. Route legality and capacity depend on complete wire, via, enclosure and spacing geometry.
  • The checked nand2_1 is three HD sites, with two 0.65 µm NMOS, two 1.0 µm high-Vt PMOS and li1 signal pins.
  • Euler trails describe connectivity through transistor edges and can suggest diffusion-sharing orders for both series and parallel networks.
