Metal Fill and Cell Fill

Cell fill is a spacer that finishes the row. Metal fill is extra metal so a density check can pass. In this tech LEF the density line is a maximum, on four metals, and it is not a current limit.

Where the numbers come from. The density lines are sky130_fd_sc_hd.tlef, the same file as lesson 30. The spacers are lesson 47.

What you will learn

  • Tell cell fill (row spacers) from metal fill (density shapes).
  • Read the tech LEF density rule: a 70% maximum on met1–met4 in a 700 µm window.
  • Include metal fill in extraction, because it changes wire capacitance.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
After step 7, routing and before step 10, tapeout, followed by a final extraction.
Tools
LibreLane’s Chip flow has KLayout filler and density steps; open_pdks has a Magic-based SKY130 fill script; OpenROAD has density_fill. Commercial: Calibre.
Why it matters
Density rules keep polishing even across the wafer, and the added metal changes wire capacitance.

1. Two fills, two jobs

In an abutting-cell flow, leftover row gaps need the approved filler/boundary treatment so that rail and well/implant continuity and spacing rules are met. A blank placement site is not automatically an electrical open if a separate power shape crosses it, but it is not automatically a legal completed layout either. The spacer that tiles the hole is cell fill: fill_1, fill_2, fill_4, and fill_8, class CORE SPACER, no transistors in the spice of fill_1 and fill_8. That is lesson 47.

Metal fill is not a cell. It is extra metal, added after the routes exist, so the fraction of a window that is covered by a layer stays inside the foundry's density rule. The spacer continues the row. The metal shapes do not have to be inside a cell at all.

2. The tech LEF gives only a maximum metal density

met1, met2, met3, and met4 each carry MAXIMUMDENSITY 70. The check window is 700 µm by 700 µm, and the window steps by 70 µm. That is a maximum: no more than 70% of the window may be that layer. The same file does not put this line on li1, on any via, or on met5. It does not publish a minimum density. The process periphery rules add pattern-density checks that are not in the tech LEF. For the aluminium back-end, rules m1.pd.1 through m4.pd.1 set a recommended minimum oxide-pattern density of 0.7, checked in 700 µm windows stepping by 70 µm (m*.pd.2a, m*.pd.2b; flags RR and A). A copper back-end option has a separate maximum of 0.77 in 50 µm windows (m1.13–m4.13, flag CU). Some quantities refer to oxide rather than metal coverage. Use the approved process deck and fill recipe; neither a missing LEF keyword nor the bare number 70% is the complete rule.

The number is also not electromigration (lesson 30). Current limits are the milliamp-per-micrometer lines. Density is how much of the window is covered. A wire can be under the current limit and still push a window over 70%, and a window can be sparse and still have one narrow wire over its current limit.

Cell fill does continue some metal: the 0.48 µm rails. That metal counts in whatever window it sits in. It is not the tool's metal-fill pass, and it does not exist on met2 and above. fill_1 has no signal pins and no devices. Adding it does not add a gate, a decap, or a spare (lesson 52, lesson 53).

met5 has no MAXIMUMDENSITY line in this file. That only states what this abstraction contains. It does not exempt met5, li1, special devices, or their surroundings from the actual process density, slotting, spacing, or exclusion rules.

3. Pattern density changes manufacturing and parasitics

A common CMP (chemical–mechanical polishing) teaching picture is that wide metal can dish and dense patterned regions can erode. The actual behavior depends on the materials and process sequence; do not assume every SKY130 metal is a copper damascene layer. Density and slotting rules also support process uniformity. Figure 2 is a generic polishing illustration, not a measured SKY130 cross-section.

The tech LEF cap is the reason that pass has a ceiling. MAXIMUMDENSITY 70 on met1 through met4, in a 700 µm window, means a window that is already full must not receive more fill. The cap is a maximum. The process deck and foundry fill recipe determine the required density range and exemptions; this LEF does not publish that complete recipe.

4. Fill belongs in the electrical model

Change near a signalElectrical consequenceReview
Floating fillCapacitive network to several neighboring conductorsDo not simply label it AC ground
Grounded fill/shieldCan reduce direct coupling while increasing ground capacitanceCheck return impedance and legal connection method
Unequal fill near a matched pairUnequal parasitic capacitance and loadingApply approved analog exclusion/symmetry treatment
Fill after timing closureDelay and slew may changeExtract and verify the filled layout

Worked example — assumed extraction result: a high-impedance node has R = 20 kΩ and C = 50 fF, so τ = RC = 1.0 ns and its one-pole frequency is about 159 MHz. If final fill adds 10 fF, τ = 1.2 ns and the pole moves to about 133 MHz. This is an electrical consequence of the assumed added capacitance, not a SKY130 fill-capacitance prediction.

Analog ↔ digital: digital timing and an analog amplifier’s pole or matching budget can both change after fill. A “density-only” edit can therefore require electrical verification. Coordinate exclusions early enough to meet both density and circuit requirements.

Check your understanding — Does absence of MAXIMUMDENSITY on met5 mean unlimited metal is legal?

No. It means that keyword is absent in this particular LEF. The approved process rules may impose other density, slotting, spacing, device-exclusion, or package constraints.

Check your understanding — a 700 µm window on met2 is already 65% dense. How much fill may be added there?

Only up to the 70% maximum in this LEF — about 5% more of the window area — and only if the process deck’s own density rules agree. The new fill also raises nearby wire capacitance, so re-extract afterward.

In the open SKY130 flow. On SKY130 shuttle chips, density fill is generated after routing by a script in open_pdks that drives Magic. LibreLane’s Chip flow has KLayout-based Filler and Density steps, and OpenROAD has a density_fill command. Whichever tool adds the fill, extract the parasitics again afterward so that timing sees the added capacitance. The usual commercial tool is Calibre.

Key takeaways

  • Cell fill is fill_1/2/4/8. It finishes the row and has no transistors.
  • Metal fill is extra metal for a density window. It is not a cell in this library.
  • MAXIMUMDENSITY 70 is a maximum on met1 through met4, in a 700 µm window stepped by 70 µm.
  • This tech LEF states no minimum density, and it does not put the cap on li1, vias, or met5.
  • 70% is not a current limit and not a target to fill up to.
  • Metal fill helps satisfy process uniformity rules and changes parasitic capacitance. Re-extract and recheck the design after final fill.
