Placement: Global, Legalization & Detailed

How OpenROAD spreads standard cells, legalizes them onto SKY130 unithd sites, and repairs slew and fanout — recorded on one 32-bit SPM at three utilizations.

Where the numbers come from. Placement, legalization and the 30/45/60 utilization table are OpenROAD logs and metrics.json from LibreLane 3.0.17 on the bundled 32-bit SPM multiplier (CLOCK_PERIOD 10 ns, sky130_fd_sc_hd, PDK sky130A). The 45% run is RUN_2026-11-11_16-09-06, taken through GDS. The 30% and 60% runs used --override-config FP_CORE_UTIL=30 and 60, tags util30 and util60, and stopped after Odb.ReportWireLength. Density bins are counted from the 45% post-detailed-placement DEF. Recorded files live in labs/analysis_examples/rtl_to_gds/. The full RTL-to-GDS walk-through is Lab 6.

What you will learn

  • Read a global-placement log: target density, overflow, HPWL and routability.
  • Legalize a placement onto unithd sites (0.46 µm × 2.72 µm) and see what detailed placement does to HPWL.
  • See how repair_design inserts buffers for slew and fanout, using the margins in the LibreLane config.
  • Compare the same netlist at FP_CORE_UTIL 30, 45 and 60 against die area, wirelength and routing DRC.

Why it matters
A congested or illegal placement makes routing and timing closure expensive. The utilization you pick here sizes the core and sets how hard the router will work.

1. After the floorplan, every cell needs a site

The floorplan has already fixed the die, the core, the unithd rows (lesson 13) and the pins. Placement’s job is to put every standard cell on a legal site: on a row, on the 0.46 µm site grid, with no overlaps, and with wires that can actually be routed. LibreLane splits that into three OpenROAD steps — global placement, a design-repair pass, and detailed placement — and that is the sequence recorded below.

On the 45% SPM the floorplan produced a die of 96.6 × 107.32 µm (area 10,367.1 µm²), a core from (5.520, 10.880) to (91.080, 95.200) µm (area 7,214.42 µm²), 31 unithd rows and 5,766 sites, with 38 I/O pins. Those numbers are the box global placement has to fill.

2. Global placement: density, overflow and HPWL

Global placement does not yet snap cells to sites. It spreads them continuously to shrink estimated wirelength and to keep local density under a target. OpenROAD’s GPL engine first solves a quadratic “InitialPlace” (conjugate gradient), then a Nesterov iteration that reports two figures at every step:

  • Overflow — how much placed area exceeds the target density, across bins. The run stops when overflow falls to about 0.1.
  • HPWL — half-perimeter wirelength, in micrometres, of a Steiner estimate. It usually rises a little as overflow is pushed down, because spreading cells to relieve density can lengthen wires.

The 45% command was:

global_placement -density 0.573812 -routability_driven -pad_right 0 -pad_left 0

PL_TARGET_DENSITY_PCT was left unset in the config. LibreLane derived the 0.573812 target from FP_CORE_UTIL=45. Routability mode was on (PL_ROUTABILITY_DRIVEN=True); timing-driven placement was off (PL_TIMING_DRIVEN=False). Cell padding was zero (DPL_CELL_PADDING=0).

At the start of GPL the log reported 52.040% utilization and a minimum feasible density of 0.5300. The engine used 16×16 bins, each 5.348 × 5.270 µm. Nesterov iteration 0 had overflow 0.8623 and HPWL 1,778.559 µm; iteration 397 had overflow 0.0990 and HPWL 3,827.500 µm. Routability mode then ran 82 iterations, reported total routing overflow 0.0000, 0 overflowed tiles, and weighted congestion 0.8653 — below the 1.0100 target — and finished successfully. Placed cell area at the end of GPL was 3,568.8119 µm².

InitialPlace, which uses a different HPWL unit, went from 7,566,590 to 3,521,926 over five conjugate-gradient iterations. Those raw counts are not micrometres; the Nesterov column is the one to quote in µm.

3. repair_design: slew, capacitance and fanout

After global placement, repair_design walks the netlist and inserts or resizes buffers so that slew, capacitance and fanout meet the design limits — with a margin, so later routing parasitics have room. The LibreLane limits on this run were MAX_FANOUT 10, MAX_CAP 0.2 pF, and DESIGN_REPAIR_MAX_SLEW_PCT / DESIGN_REPAIR_MAX_CAP_PCT of 20. The log of 32-openroad-repairdesignpostgpl reads:

+ repair_design -verbose -max_wire_length 0.0 -slew_margin 20.0 -cap_margin 20.0
[INFO RSZ-0027] Inserted 34 sky130_fd_sc_hd__clkdlybuf4s25_1 input buffers.
[INFO RSZ-0028] Inserted 1 sky130_fd_sc_hd__clkdlybuf4s25_1 output buffers.
[INFO RSZ-0034] Found 2 slew violations.
[INFO RSZ-0035] Found 2 fanout violations.
[INFO RSZ-0038] Inserted 25 buffers in 2 nets.

At the end of that step the “Timing Repair Buffer” class had 60 cells, area 573.05 µm². Later CTS and hold repair add more: the final 45% metrics list 93 timing-repair buffers (area 903.366 µm²), including 33 dlygate4sd3_1 hold cells and 52 clkdlybuf4s25_1, plus 9 clkbuf_16 and 64 dfrtp_2. The hold-cell story is in lesson 45; it is not repeated here.

4. Legalization and detailed placement

Detailed placement snaps every cell onto a unithd site (0.46 µm wide, 2.72 µm tall, the HD row pitch of lesson 13) and removes overlaps. The command was detailed_placement -max_displacement 500 100. On the 45% SPM, this step’s own displacement was already zero — the cells were legal before the optimizer ran. Legalization still moved them enough to change HPWL:

  • Original HPWL 5,294.5 µm.
  • Legalized HPWL 5,583.9 µm (+5%).
  • After the detailed-placement optimize pass: 5,294.5 µm again (delta −5.2%).

The same pattern appeared at 30% (5,519.2 → 5,850.5 → 5,519.2 µm, +6% then −5.7%) and at 60% (5,610.6 → 5,915.0 → 5,610.6 µm, +5% then −5.1%). Legalization makes the placement site-legal, which costs a little HPWL; the optimizer then gives most of it back. Instance count at 45% DPL was 381, utilization 0.553243. Final displacement figures in metrics.json (max 23.3 µm, mean 0.677, total 328.658) include later CTS and repair moves, and are not the DPL-step displacement of zero.

5. A utilization sweep: 30, 45 and 60%

The same SPM netlist was run at three FP_CORE_UTIL values. Lower utilization asks LibreLane for a larger core, so the same cells sit farther apart; higher utilization packs them into a smaller die. Three observations, all from these runs, not from a general model:

  • 45% produced the shortest detailed-route wirelength (6,508 µm). At 30% the larger die stretched the longest net to 185.66 µm and total WL to 6,613 µm. At 60% congestion forced detours: WL rose to 7,613 µm even though the longest net shrank to 139.72 µm on the smaller die, and the router used much more met3 (286 µm) and met4 (414 µm).
  • Tighter utilization made the first routing iteration harder — 95 DRC errors at 60% versus 19 at 45% — and the 60% run went up from 15 to 21 errors at iteration 2 before closing. It still closed.
  • The 60% minimum feasible density was 0.7000 against a target of 0.7223: there was little spare room. Mid-routability GPL reported 1 overflowed tile (0.64%) and weighted congestion 0.9930, still under the 1.0100 target, and routability finished successfully. Final GRT overflow was 0.

Final instance utilization is higher than FP_CORE_UTIL because CTS, repair buffers and fill cells are added after the core is sized. At 45% the final metrics list 1,226 instances, of which 803 are fill (area 2,667.56 µm²), 99 are taps, 423 are standard cells (area 4,546.86 µm²), and utilization is 0.630246.

6. A density map of the 45% placement

OpenROAD GPL used a 16×16 bin grid on this design. Counting PLACED components in the post-detailed-placement DEF, in those same bin sizes, gives a picture of how evenly the movable cells were spread.

The map is an instance count, not an area-weighted density, and it omits fill (fill is inserted after routing). Empty bins at the south edge are the core margin. The placement is even enough that no bin exceeds three of these cells — consistent with GPL reporting zero overflowed tiles and a comfortable gap between 52% utilization and a 0.57 target.

7. What to check before you route

Before handing the database to CTS and routing, the logs above are the checklist:

  • GPL overflow at or under ~0.1, routability finished, overflowed tiles at or near zero.
  • Utilization and target density with some margin — the 60% run’s 0.7000 feasible versus 0.7223 target is the tight end of this sweep.
  • repair_design has cleared slew, capacitance and fanout at the configured margins.
  • Detailed placement is legal on the library site, and the HPWL penalty of legalization has been recovered or accepted.

Routing congestion, layer usage and the DRC-iteration story are lesson 59. Multi-corner slacks after route are lesson 45. The GDS, DRC and LVS of the 45% run are Lab 6 and lesson 62.

Why was detailed-route wirelength shortest at 45%, not at 30%?

A larger core (30%) makes some pins farther apart, so the longest net and the total length grow even though congestion is lower (GRT usage 11.71%). A tighter core (60%) shortens the longest net but the router has to detour and climb to met3 and met4, and total length grows. On this SPM, 45% sat in between: GRT usage 15.60%, almost no met3/met4, and the shortest DRT total (6,508 µm). That is a measured result for this netlist and this clock, not a rule that 45% is always best.
