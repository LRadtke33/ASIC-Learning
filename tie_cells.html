Lesson 50 / 84 · Part VI, Physical cells and the floorplan

Tie Cells

A logic constant still needs an electrical implementation. A tie cell gives the physical flow an explicit constant-net driver, a power-domain association, and a place to manage fanout and routing.

Where the numbers come from. The example is sky130_fd_sc_hd__conb_1. Its raw SPICE model, physical abstract, and Liberty file describe different aspects of the same cell; none should be mistaken for the others.

What you will learn

  • Explain why a logic constant still needs an electrical driver.
  • Use conb_1, which provides both HI and LO outputs, within its limits.
  • Treat a constant as conditional on its supply and power state.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

Why it matters
A constant still needs a real driver in the correct power domain.

1. A tie-high and a tie-low are output pins

Unused logic inputs, mode controls, and permanently disabled enables often need a constant. RTL can express that with 1'b0 or 1'b1. During implementation, the flow maps the constant to the approved tie cell. The resulting net has a known output pin instead of an informal connection to a power stripe.

This is a library and flow convention with real electrical consequences. Do not conclude that tying a MOS gate to a rail is universally forbidden, or that every process uses the same tie-cell circuit. Some libraries use active devices to isolate the gate from supply disturbances; others use different approved structures. Use the cell provided for the domain and process.

In the HD library, conb_1 is a three-site cell, 1.38 × 2.72 µm, with area 3.7536 µm². It offers both constants:

2. A model name does not prove the physical device

The upstream schematic lists two instances of short: one between VGND and LO, and one between HI and VPWR, each also referencing VNB. It uses width and length parameters w=480000u and l=45000u. In this library’s model-unit convention the numerical values correspond to 0.48 and 0.045 in micrometers. A suffix in an instance line cannot be interpreted without the model’s expected units.

The published extraction discussion for this cell reports a poly-resistor representation of these links. The safe lesson is to distinguish a schematic/LVS device abstraction from a physical junction. In particular, ANTENNADIFFAREA 0.0001 is data supplied to an antenna checker; it does not establish that a measured 0.0001 µm² protective diffusion is present. Read the installed view, device definitions, and extracted layout together. [source schematic] [upstream extraction discrepancy]

The typical Liberty cell_leakage_power is 0.003240037 nW in the library’s units. That is characterized cell metadata. It is not the maximum current that a tie output can supply, an ESD rating, or a model for operation while its domain is unpowered.

3. Capacitance is only one of the limits

A tie net can have many sink gates, substantial routing capacitance, and a long antenna exposure. Physical flows often replicate ties near their loads. This reduces wire length and makes fanout, power-domain assignment, antenna repair, and later ECOs easier to control. [OpenROAD tie-fanout repair]

As a hand check, 100 inputs each having an assumed 2.30 fF add to 230 fF = 0.230 pF, before wire capacitance. That is below the listed HI maximum of 1.9038 pF. It does not establish that using one tie for those 100 sinks is permitted: a flow’s fanout, separation, domain, and antenna constraints can require many local ties. There are no combinational delay tables in this simple conb_1 JSON from which to infer analog settling or output resistance.

4. Power states make a “constant” conditional

Analog ↔ digital: an analog enable tied to HI is only high when the tie’s supply domain is valid. If an always-on controller depends on a tie located in a switched domain, powering that domain down can leave the controller input undefined or create a back-power path. Isolation, level shifting, or an always-on tie may be needed. The constant logic function does not model every unpowered electrical condition.

The current example flow identifies sky130_fd_sc_hd__conb_1/HI and sky130_fd_sc_hd__conb_1/LO through SYNTH_TIEHI_CELL and SYNTH_TIELO_CELL. Older OpenLane configurations used SYNTH_TIEHI_PORT and SYNTH_TIELO_PORT. Use the syntax for the installed version. [configuration]

Check your understanding — Does passing max_capacitance prove a tie net is complete?

No. Also check fanout/separation requirements, signal and supply connectivity, voltage/power states, antenna rules, and the installed cell models. Capacitance is one check.

Check your understanding — why not connect an unused gate input directly to the VPWR rail?

A gate tied straight to a rail has no device between its thin oxide and the supply, so a disturbance on the rail reaches the gate directly. The flow also loses the named constant net it uses for power-domain, fanout and timing checks. A tie cell gives the constant a driver in the intended domain.

Key takeaways

  • A tie cell implements a constant as a named library output in the intended power domain.
  • The HD example conb_1 is three sites with HI and LO outputs.
  • The raw short model and antenna metadata do not establish an ideal wire, an active protection circuit, or a physical diffusion measurement.
  • Large Liberty load limits do not authorize unlimited fanout or long chip-wide constant nets.
  • A logic constant depends on the electrical state of its supply; review power sequencing and isolation.
