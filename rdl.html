Lesson 67 / 84 · Part VII, The chip boundary

RDL Routing

Redistribution routing connects a die’s electrical pad to a package attachment point. The electrical access shape, passivation opening, redistribution metal, and bump are different objects.

Where the numbers come from. The HD routing LEF stops at met5. SKY130’s broader documentation also includes RDL and WLCSP rules, so absence from that LEF is not evidence that the process documentation has no redistribution layer.

What you will learn

  • Separate a LEF PAD pin, a passivation opening, a redistribution trace and a bump.
  • Find SKY130’s RDL and WLCSP rules outside the core routing LEF.
  • Estimate the width, resistance and inductance of a redistribution route.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
Package planning, alongside step 4, floorplan and power grid and before step 10, tapeout.
Tools
Bump maps and redistribution routing come from the package vendor or a package-design tool such as Cadence Allegro Package Designer.
Why it matters
Redistribution routing adds resistance and inductance in series with every pad it serves.

Lesson 6 introduced flip-chip and wafer-level packages and the redistribution layer that routes perimeter pads to bump sites. This lesson stays with SKY130’s own files and asks what each of them does — and does not — establish about that connection.

1. A PAD pin rectangle is not automatically a bond opening

The checked top_gpiov2 LEF has an 80 × 200 µm outline. Its PAD electrical access shape is a met5 rectangle from x = 32.115 to 53.655 and y = 125.470 to 147.015 µm. Subtraction gives 21.540 × 21.545 µm. Its upper edge is 200 − 147.015 = 52.985 µm below the upper cell boundary. [pinned GPIO LEF]

These are physical-abstract coordinates for routing access. They do not identify the complete metal pad, the passivation opening, a bondable finish, or an approved wire-bond/bump landing area. Those require the relevant GDS layers, pad/overlay cells, process option, and package assembly rules. A router’s pin marker is not a package drawing.

2. The process and package documents contain more than the core LEF

The HD tech LEF defines li1 plus met1 through met5. In contrast, the broader SKY130 rules contain an rdl section and separate wafer-level chip-scale package (WLCSP) tables. The correct conclusion is that the core routing abstraction omits those layers, not that no RDL option exists. [HD LEF] [process RDL rules] [WLCSP rules]

As a clearly scoped example, the published Amkor WLCSP table lists 400 µm and 500 µm bump-pitch variants and 10 µm minimum RDL width and spacing. Those numbers apply to the named table and its supported flows. The DECA table and other process rules have their own conditions; do not merge convenient values from several options. Documentation of an option also does not establish its availability for a particular shuttle or packaging order.

3. Follow the electrical path through the I/O circuitry

Some GPIO pins are controls or receiver outputs, not direct wires to the pad. For example, OUT controls the output driver and IN comes from the input receiver. Analog access pins can take direct or resistor-protected paths according to the I/O guide. A diagram from “core” to “pad” therefore needs an I/O-circuit block, not a single unexplained metal short. [I/O guide]

4. Crossing, resistance, and return current are physical constraints

Two different nets cannot simply cross in the same conducting layer. They need a different route, an additional insulated routing layer with legal vias, or a different pin/bump assignment. RDL stacks can have one or several layers; “RDL” does not inherently mean one layer. Allowed corner shapes and fillets are process-specific, so a 45° jog is not an automatic solution to every collision.

Worked routing-width example: using the cited Amkor table’s 10 µm minimum width and 10 µm spacing, three straight parallel tracks require at least 3 × 10 + 2 × 10 = 50 µm between their outer edges, before keepouts and capture-pad geometry. This is a local width calculation only; it does not establish routability through a bump array.

Worked electrical example — assumed package model: a 2 mm RDL route with 0.20 Ω resistance drops 10 mV at 50 mA. An assumed 1 nH shared connection experiencing a 20 mA change in 1 ns develops L di/dt = 20 mV. Neither resistance nor inductance is specified by the HD LEF; package extraction or a qualified model supplies them.

Analog ↔ digital: supply and return paths in the package can convert simultaneous switching into analog-reference disturbance. Plan signal/return geometry, power/ground bump locations, ESD discharge paths, and mechanical restrictions together. A shorter geometric path is not automatically a better low-noise path if it lacks a nearby return.

Check your understanding — Does a missing RDL layer in the HD LEF prove SKY130 has no RDL rules?

No. The core routing LEF is one abstraction. The broader process documentation includes RDL and WLCSP rules, whose applicability and availability must be checked for the selected manufacturing/package option.

Check your understanding — a 2 mm redistribution trace is 10 µm wide. How many squares is it, and why does that matter?

2000 µm / 10 µm = 200 squares, so its resistance is 200 times the layer’s sheet resistance. Long, narrow redistribution runs add resistance in series with every pad they serve, which matters most for supplies and fast outputs.

Key takeaways

  • A LEF PAD access rectangle is not a passivation opening or an approved bond target.
  • The HD routing LEF stops at met5, while broader SKY130 documentation contains RDL/WLCSP options.
  • Use one applicable process/package rule set, including its supported-flow conditions.
  • RDL may have one or several layers; separate nets need insulation if they cross.
  • Routing resistance, package inductance, return currents, ESD, and mechanical requirements belong in the same interface review.
