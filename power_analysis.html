Power Analysis & Low-Power Cells

OpenSTA report_power on the Lab 6 SPM: internal, switching and leakage at nine corners, the clock tree’s share after CTS, and the SKY130 lpflow cells a real flow will not let ABC use as ordinary logic.

Where the numbers come from. Every watt figure is from OpenSTA report_power on LibreLane 3.0.17 run RUN_2026-11-11_16-09-06 of the bundled 32-bit SPM (sky130_fd_sc_hd, CLOCK_PERIOD 10 ns), 11 November 2026, America/Chicago. The nine post-PnR corner reports are under step 54-openroad-stapostpnr; copies, plus the nom_tt pre-PnR and post-CTS reports, are in labs/analysis_examples/power/. LibreLane called report_power -corner with no VCD and no SAIF, so OpenSTA used its built-in default switching activity — these are not vector-accurate numbers for a particular testbench. The power keys in metrics.json match the max_ff_n40C_1v95 report (1.484736 mW total). Cell areas in section 6 are from this PDK’s HD LEF. No UPF was applied, and IR drop was not executed on this run.

What you will learn

  • Read an OpenSTA report_power table: internal, switching and leakage, by sequential, combinational and clock groups.
  • Quote the SPM’s post-PnR power at nom_tt and across the nine corners, with the activity caveat.
  • See the clock tree appear in the power report at CTS.
  • Name the SKY130 lpflow isolation, keep-alive and level-shifter cells, and why LibreLane excludes them from synthesis — the worked example is lesson 44.

Why it matters
A chip can meet timing and still be too hot, too hungry, or incorrectly isolated. The report is how you see that before tapeout — if you know what was assumed about activity.

1. Three kinds of power, one formula

Lesson 37 already uses the budget model P ≈ αCV²f — average 0-to-1 events per cycle, times capacitance, times voltage squared, times frequency. That is the switching term. A Liberty-based report_power adds two more:

  • Internal (sometimes “dynamic internal”): short-circuit current and the energy of charging capacitance inside the cell, taken from the Liberty internal_power tables. It is often the largest term, as it is on this SPM.
  • Leakage: static current from the Liberty leakage_power tables, strongly dependent on temperature, voltage and threshold. On this HD run at 25 °C it is almost invisible; at 100 °C ss it is still a small fraction of the total, but it is no longer zero in the report’s rounding.

Glitch power (extra transitions that a functional testbench may not show) is in the switching term only if the activity file captured those edges. This run had no activity file, so glitch is not separately visible.

Dynamic power tracks voltage: ff at 1.95 V is the highest total (1.485 mW), ss at 1.60 V the lowest (1.008–1.022 mW). Leakage does the opposite of the usual “fast = leaky” intuition on this particular table because ss is at 100 °C and ff is at −40 °C — temperature wins, and ss leakage is 2.90 µW, about 0.3% of that corner’s total, against 7.46 nW at cold ff. The exact values are in the nine power.rpt files; the table rounds to 0.001 mW for the dynamic columns. metrics.json’s power__total is 0.001484736 W, which is the max_ff row.

A VCD from a testbench would change the activity, and therefore the internal and switching columns, at every corner. That experiment was not run. What you can already trust is the ratio of corners under the same default activity, and the fact that leakage at 100 °C is three orders of magnitude above leakage at −40 °C on this netlist.

4. The clock tree shows up at CTS

Before placement, OpenSTA still sees an ideal clock: the Clock group is 0.000 W (pre-PnR nom_tt total 0.855 mW). After CTS (step 36) the tree has real buffers and real pins, and the Clock group is 0.234 mW, 20.3% of 1.151 mW. After detailed route and RCX the total settles at 1.275 mW, clock 19.5%. Clock gating, which is how you cut that term on a quiet block, is lesson 37; this run did not gate the SPM’s clock.

5. Default activity is not a testbench

LibreLane’s STA script (librelane/scripts/openroad/sta/corner.tcl) calls report_power -corner $corner_name and writes the four metrics. It does not call read_vcd or read_saif. OpenSTA then applies its built-in default switching activity to every pin that has no annotated activity. The 1.275 mW figure is that calculation, at 10 ns, on this netlist and these parasitics. It is honest, and it is not “the SPM’s power in operation.” A later lab that dumps a VCD from the SPM’s own testbench and re-runs report_power would be the right way to put a use-case number on the same design. That was not done here.

6. Low-power cells, and why synthesis must not invent them

The HD library in this PDK contains 34 cells whose names begin with lpflow_. They are for a power-intent flow (UPF or a similar description): isolation at a domain crossing, a keep-alive buffer on always-on rails (KAPWR), a decap on that rail, a bleeder, and level-shifters that sit in an isolated well (the lsbuf cells are 5.44 µm tall — two HD rows, 2 × 2.72 µm — because of that well). All 34 are on LibreLane’s PNR_EXCLUDED_CELL_FILE (drc_exclude.cells). Lesson 44 is the worked example: on the raw Liberty, ABC used lpflow_inputiso1p_1 and lpflow_isobufsrc_1 as ordinary datapath gates; after the default lists, it did not. A UPF file would insert those cells at domain crossings, with their sleep pins tied to a control network — that is a different step, and it was not run on the SPM. This lesson does not claim a qualified SKY130 power-gating recipe. Level shifters as a library topic are lesson 54; I/O IR drop is lesson 68.

UPF (IEEE 1801) is the usual language for domains, isolation, retention and level-shifting in a commercial flow. The open SKY130 flow LibreLane ran here has no UPF on the SPM: one supply pair (VPWR/VGND), no isolation cells in the netlist, no retention. Multi-Vt comparison (HD vs HDLL leakage) is often quoted as a several-fold difference in the SkyWater documentation; that ratio was not measured in this run and is not tabulated here.

7. What this run did not include

IR-drop analysis was enabled (RUN_IRDROP_REPORT was true in resolved.json) but was not executed in that run, because the signoff resume started at Magic.StreamOut — the same fact as lesson 8. A later OpenROAD analysis on the same final/ ODB and nom SPEF, in lesson 63, measured 132 µV worst VPWR drop and 1.28 mW total power, in agreement with this lesson’s STA report_power of 1.275 mW at nom_tt. A VCD-annotated report_power, a clock-gated variant of the SPM, and a UPF isolation insertion are later experiments, not results of this lesson.

The nom_tt report says leakage is 0.0% of 1.275 mW. Is leakage zero?

No. The same report’s leakage column is 4.004×10−9 W, 4.00 nW, which is 0.0003% of the total and rounds to 0.0% in the percentage line. At ss_100C_1v60 the same netlist leaks 2.90 µW, 0.3% of that corner. Read the watt column, not only the rounded percentage.

Why is the SPM’s clock-group power zero before placement and about a fifth of the total after CTS?

Pre-PnR STA uses an ideal clock: no tree, no clock buffers, no real clock-pin capacitance in the Clock group. CTS inserts the tree (this run’s clock buffers are in the metrics as nine clock-buffer instances). After that, OpenSTA has a real clock network to cost, and at nom_tt it is 0.234 mW of 1.151 mW (20.3%). The post-route figure is 0.249 mW of 1.275 mW (19.5%).
