Fill Cells

What fills the empty sites between standard cells, and why the gaps can't just stay empty.

What you will learn

  • Explain why placements leave gaps, and why gaps must still be filled.
  • Tile any whole-site gap with fill_1, fill_2, fill_4 and fill_8.
  • Tell spacer fill cells from decap cells, which add capacitance and leakage.

1. Gaps are inevitable

In the previous topic the cells were drawn packed tightly together, but a real design never fills its rows 100%. The placer deliberately targets a lower utilization (often 60–80%) so the router has room to work, so cells can be legalized without long displacements, and so late ECO fixes have somewhere to go. The result: after placement, every row has leftover empty sites scattered between the cells.

2. Why an empty site is a problem

An empty site isn't neutral — it's a hole in layers that are required to be continuous along the row:

  • Rail continuity. Cell rails may stop at a gap unless special-route metal bridges it. Filler cells continue the rail template and must be connected consistently with the PDN.
  • Well and implant geometry. Gaps can create well/implant notches, spacing violations or unintended islands. Separate wells are legal when the PDK permits them and each region is correctly biased; “all wells must be continuous” is not a universal rule.
  • Density and pattern rules. Applicable layers have their own window-based rules. Standard-cell fillers restore row geometry; dedicated metal or device fill may still be needed. Filling every site does not by itself satisfy density checks.

3. Fill cells: cells with nothing inside

A basic fill cell has no logic function and no signal pins. It normally carries the row’s rail, well and implant geometry. It can have explicit power pins: the SKY130 HD fillers below expose VGND and VPWR in LEF. Do not confuse “no signal pin” with “no pins.”

Fillers come in a small family of widths, typically powers of two: FILL1, FILL2, FILL4, FILL8, FILL16… With those widths, any gap can be tiled exactly — the tool scans each row and drops in the largest filler that fits, repeating until every site is occupied (a 5-site gap becomes FILL4 + FILL1).

Fill insertion occurs after the placement is sufficiently settled; the exact stage is flow-dependent. Tools may remove and reinsert fillers around routing, timing repair or ECO changes. Many libraries also provide decap cells to use some whitespace for supply decoupling. They have electrical devices and leakage, so they are not interchangeable with empty spacers in every verification view.

4. SkyWater's fillers, and the decap cells that are not fillers

sky130_fd_sc_hd has four spacer cells: fill_1, fill_2, fill_4, and fill_8. There is no fill_16 in this library. A 16-site gap is two fill_8 cells. A 5-site gap is still fill_4 plus fill_1, the tiling in section 3.

Each LEF says CLASS CORE SPACER and SITE unithd. The width is the suffix times 0.46 µm. fill_1, read in full, has two pins, VGND and VPWR, and both are the met1 rails plus a local-interconnect strip on the same edge. No signal pin. The SPICE netlists of fill_1 and fill_8 contain no transistors. The rail and the well are the whole cell, which is what Figure 2 was drawing.

The decoupling cells are a different family: decap_3, decap_4, decap_6, decap_8, and decap_12. The LEF class is CORE, not SPACER. They occupy 3, 4, 6, 8, and 12 sites. Each SPICE netlist has two transistors, and the widths do not change with the cell: a high-Vt PMOS of 0.87 µm and an NMOS of 0.55 µm. The length does change. The PMOS gate is tied to VGND and both channel ends sit on VPWR. The NMOS gate is tied to VPWR and both channel ends sit on VGND. That is a capacitor between the rails, drawn with long channels because the row is only 2.72 µm tall and the devices cannot grow upward.

Logic transistors in this library are 0.15 µm long. These decap channels are 0.59 µm to 4.73 µm. The Liberty file for decap_4 at the typical corner records area 5.0048 µm², which is 1.84 × 2.72, and cell leakage 0.00324 nW. It does not publish a pin capacitance. The Liberty file gives no capacitance value for it. The longer channel is the bigger capacitor. How many femtofarads that is comes from a device model, not from the LEF outline.

decap_4 still draws the same 0.48 µm met1 rails as fill_1 and inv_1, out to 0.24 µm past the top and bottom edges. A decap abuts a logic cell and continues the power stripe. It also adds the two MOS capacitors. A filler only continues the stripe. The decap family is lesson 52. The endcap the flow inserts is decap_3, in lesson 49.

5. Physical cells and the netlist used for verification

After placement, the row still has jobs that are not logic. A fill cell continues the rails and the well. A tap ties the well and the wafer (lesson 48). A decap is a capacitor between the rails (lesson 52). diode_2 is the antenna diode (lesson 51).

These cells are usually inserted by physical-design steps rather than by Boolean synthesis. Their presence in exported netlists depends on the view and flow: a logical timing netlist may omit fillers, whereas a physical/LVS netlist must represent or consistently filter the electrically relevant structures. Antenna diodes attach to signal nets and decaps contain devices. They must not be discarded simply because they are not logic gates.

Worked checkpoint — Fill a thirteen-site gap

Assume an otherwise legal gap of 13 ordinary HD sites and compatible filler boundaries. One exact cover is fill_8 + fill_4 + fill_1, since 8 + 4 + 1 = 13. Its width is 13 × 0.46 = 5.98 µm. This checks coverage only: tap spacing, special boundaries, decap planning and power connectivity remain separate requirements.

Check your understanding — why can a decap cell not fill every gap that a spacer can?

The smallest decap, decap_3, is three sites wide, so one- and two-site gaps still need spacer cells. Decaps also add leakage current, so a flow may limit how many it inserts.

In the open SKY130 flow. OpenROAD’s filler_placement fills every remaining gap after detailed routing with the cells it is given. LibreLane’s FillInsertion step passes it the library’s decap and fill cells. Because fillers have no logic they are left out of the netlist used for timing, but they stay in the layout that goes to DRC and LVS.

filler_placement {sky130_fd_sc_hd__decap_* sky130_fd_sc_hd__fill_*}

Key takeaways

  • Real placements leave empty sites on purpose (routability, legalization, ECO space).
  • Gaps can interrupt row geometry; fillers and PDN routing restore the intended template, while DRC and connectivity checks verify it.
  • The checked HD spacers use widths of 1, 2, 4 and 8 sites and can tile integral-site gaps when boundary and placement rules permit.
  • These SKY130 HD filler examples have single-row height and integral-site widths. Placement applies the cell’s LEF origin, allowed symmetry and row orientation so compatible boundaries abut.
  • Filler insertion and reinsertion are flow-dependent; decaps add supply capacitance with leakage and reliability tradeoffs.
  • This library's spacers are fill_1/2/4/8. The decaps are decap_3/4/6/8/12, with a 0.87 µm high-Vt PMOS and a 0.55 µm NMOS whose channel gets longer as the cell gets wider.
  • Physical-design cells are placed according to their electrical roles, spacing rules and flow. Exported netlists must preserve electrically relevant connectivity, including decoupling and antenna devices where required.
