Clock Gating & Power States

Safe clock pulses, test access, analog readiness, and coherent shutdown and restart.

What you will learn
  • Distinguish register enables, integrated clock gating, and removal of power.
  • Explain the latch phase, enable timing, and test-control requirements of a real SKY130 clock gate.
  • Estimate a clock-load power budget without confusing it with measured chip power.
  • Design a reviewable sequence for clocks, reset, isolation, analog readiness, and restarted communication.

Where this fits · the flow map is lesson 8

In the flow
Clock gating is written in step 2, RTL and simulation and mapped in step 3, synthesis; power gating is planned from step 1, specification and built in step 4, floorplan and power grid.
Tools
Yosys maps clock-gating cells. Commercial flows describe power domains in a UPF file used by Genus or Design Compiler and by Innovus or IC Compiler II.
Why it matters
Gating clocks and supplies saves power but adds timing checks and wake-up sequences that have to be designed.

1. Stopping updates is not the same as stopping the clock

A register enable says whether a state value changes. Its clock pin may continue switching on every cycle. An integrated clock gate prevents selected clock pulses from reaching a group of registers. Power gating disconnects a supply through an appropriate switch structure. These mechanisms have different effects on state, leakage, wake latency, verification, and the surrounding analog circuitry.

For a capacitive node, a useful switching estimate is P ≈ αCV²f, where α counts average 0-to-1 charging events per reference cycle. A continuously running clock has one charging event per cycle under this convention. The expression excludes leakage and does not fully represent internal cell current or short-circuit power. It is a budget model, not a substitute for characterized power analysis.

In lesson 30's executed synthesis example, an RTL enable mapped to edfxtp data-enable pins while the original clock still drove the flip-flops. No separate clock gate appeared. A flow must deliberately infer, insert, or instantiate a suitable integrated clock-gating (ICG) cell and preserve its intended clock semantics. The Yosys clockgate documentation describes one available transformation; its existence does not mean every invocation of synth uses it.

2. Why an ordinary AND gate is insufficient

For a positive-edge clock, the expression gclk = clk & enable seems natural: enable one passes the clock, and enable zero holds the output low. But if enable changes while the clock is high, the output can rise at the enable edge or fall before the clock falls. A downstream flip-flop can see an unintended active edge, an inadequate pulse width, or a clock relationship that the intended synchronous timing model never allowed.

The problem is timing, not the Boolean truth table. A positive-edge ICG typically holds its enable constant throughout the high clock phase. A latch transparent during the low phase can update the enable while the output clock is safely low, then retain that decision during the next high phase. A falling-edge flip-flop can implement a related gating strategy, but its timing and available enable window differ; use the architecture and cell recognized by the implementation flow.

“Glitch-free” is conditional on the cell's operating and timing requirements. An asynchronous enable can still arrive near the latch-closing boundary and violate its constraints. Synchronize or otherwise qualify a wake request in a running control domain before it controls the gate. Also define the initial gate state: a gate without a reset input needs a valid clock/enable initialization sequence, not an assumed power-up latch value.

3. What the SKY130 cells actually provide

The pinned HD library contains sky130_fd_sc_hd__dlclkp_1 and its scan-capable counterpart sky130_fd_sc_hd__sdlclkp_1. Their public functional models and Liberty records identify the behavior below. These are exact facts about the cited views, not an assertion that any use of those cells is automatically timing-correct.

CellLogical control pinsLiberty classificationRelevant checks
dlclkp_1CLK, GATE → GCLKlatch_posedgeGATE setup/hold to CLK; clock minimum pulse width; CLK-to-GCLK delay
sdlclkp_1CLK, GATE, SCE → GCLKlatch_posedge_precontrolGATE and SCE setup/hold to CLK; clock pulse width; output loading and slew

The term latch_posedge classifies a gate used with a positive-edge clock; it does not mean the internal enable latch is transparent while CLK is high. The functional model explicitly inverts CLK before driving a positive-level latch. Its state is therefore updated while CLK is low.

For the scan-capable gate, SCE is combined with GATE before the latch. When SCE is asserted with valid timing, clocks can pass even if functional GATE is low. SCE itself has timing checks; it is not permission to force an arbitrary output pulse. Test planning must establish mode controls before the relevant shift or capture clocks. A scan chain behind a permanently closed gate cannot shift.

Use the appropriate functional and timing models for verification, and the characterized cell in implementation. A generic latch-plus-AND RTL model can illustrate behavior, but it does not supply the cell's pulse-width limits, internal power, parasitics, or a completed clock-tree strategy. A library parser may also treat a clock gate's state-table representation differently from ordinary combinational functions; check tool support rather than silently replacing it with a buffer.

4. A clock-power and loading calculation

Worked budget: the cited SKY130 HD dfxtp_1 TT/25°C/1.8 V file reports 1.794 fF at CLK. For 512 such clock pins, the pin-only load is 512 × 1.794 fF = 0.918528 pF. Assume another 0.600 pF of effective downstream wire and buffer load. The illustrative total is 1.518528 pF.

Pclock ≈ 1.518528 pF × (1.8 V)² × 100 MHz = 0.4920 mW

If that entire downstream capacitance switches only 25% of the time, its ideal average switching term becomes 0.1230 mW, a reduction of 0.3690 mW. The gate and upstream tree still consume power; cell internal power, enable activity, data activity, leakage, and wake transients are omitted. These assumptions do not predict a measured ASIC saving.

Loading also constrains the topology. The cited dlclkp_1 TT output maximum capacitance is 0.152903 pF, far below the 0.918528 pF of those 512 clock pins even before routing. One tiny gate cannot be treated as a direct driver for the entire example bank. Clock buffers, branch sizing, transition limits, skew, and physical placement must be designed together. See the clock-tree lesson.

Clock gating can reduce interference during a sensitive analog measurement, but resuming many branches at once can create a current step, supply disturbance, and reference error. Schedule quiet intervals and restart behavior around the actual sampling contract. A lower average power number does not establish a lower worst-case disturbance.

5. The controller must remain able to wake the block

A block cannot rely solely on logic driven by its stopped clock to reopen that same clock. Keep wake detection and the necessary control state in an always-on or otherwise running domain. A useful protocol requests quiescence, waits for the block to finish or explicitly abandon outstanding work, then closes the gate. A wake request is retained long enough for the control domain to observe it and reopen the gate.

The gated clock is related to its parent clock. Do not declare them asynchronous just to remove difficult paths. Ensure the timing flow propagates the parent clock through the recognized gate, or creates the appropriate generated-clock relationship when required. Check clock-gating setup/hold, minimum high/low pulse width, insertion delay, clock slew, and skew after the tree is built. The OpenSTA reference documents gating checks and generated clocks; implementation-specific clock discovery still needs inspection.

Reset release also needs clock edges. If a local reset synchronizer is clocked by the gated leaf clock, the controller must let that clock run while reset is released. Waiting for “reset complete” before permitting the very edges needed to complete reset creates a deadlock. Separate reset assertion, which may be asynchronous, from release and initialization, which require the selected clocks and supplies to be valid.

6. Clock gating, power gating, and retention

MechanismWhat can remain valid?What it does not guarantee
Register enableStored value while powered; clock may continueElimination of clock switching
Clock gatingOrdinary static register state while supply remains validZero leakage, analog bias shutdown, or safe power removal
Power gating without retentionOnly state outside the removed supply domainPreservation of local state or valid outputs
Power gating with retentionSpecifically supported state on a valid retention supplyRetention of every node, correct save/restore, or coherent live protocols

When a domain loses power, ordinary outputs can become invalid even if ideal RTL still displays zero or one. Isolation must prevent that invalid state from affecting powered receivers. The isolation control and necessary clamp circuitry must themselves remain powered. Choose clamp polarity from the interface: forcing an active-low request to zero could assert a request rather than make the interface idle. Voltage translation and isolation are separate functions unless a validated cell combines them.

Retention requires a supported cell or macro, valid power connectivity, and a verified save/restore sequence. This lesson does not claim a particular SKY130 retention or power-switch solution is qualified. The HD library does, however, contain cells that isolation and keep-alive nets use. In the bundled tt_025C_1v80 Liberty file they are named lpflow_inputiso0n, lpflow_inputiso0p, lpflow_inputiso1n, lpflow_inputiso1p, lpflow_isobufsrc, lpflow_clkbufkapwr, lpflow_clkinvkapwr and lpflow_decapkapwr. The SkyWater library notes also describe sleep transistors in the ls and lp libraries and state-retention flops in the ms library. For any implementation, use the actual power-intent methodology, device limits, library views, and physical checks. Power gating is not accomplished by assigning a Verilog signal named power_good.

7. Make power transitions a protocol

During planned shutdown, stop new work, drain or explicitly cancel in-flight transactions, assert the required isolation, and establish reset/clock conditions before removing power. During restart, establish the supply and power-good condition, run the clocks needed for reset release and initialization, restore or rebuild state, and wait for the analog readiness contract. Remove isolation and admit traffic only when the receiving interface can interpret the outputs correctly.

Power-good and analog-ready are different conditions. A supply may be inside its allowed window while a reference, bias loop, oscillator, or sampled output is still settling. Conversely, a sudden supply failure may leave no time for an orderly shutdown. Define the emergency behavior separately: clamp or invalidate outputs promptly, reset affected protocols, and record or report the loss according to system requirements.

The OpenTitan power-manager specification is a concrete primary example of coordinating always-on control, clock control, reset, isolation, and startup handshakes. Its sequence is specific to that design; Figure 3 is an independent teaching sequence, not an OpenTitan or SKY130 implementation recipe.

A simple energy break-even estimate can inform whether a short idle interval justifies full power removal. With assumed combined entry/exit energy of 1 nJ and a 10 µW saving while off, the energy-only break-even interval is 1 nJ / 10 µW = 100 µs. Wake latency, reference settling, retained-state energy, and reliability constraints can require a longer interval. These numbers are illustrative and were not extracted from a SKY130 block.

8. Restart the communication state coherently

Resetting only one side of a live request/acknowledge channel can create a missing request, stale acknowledgment, duplicate delivery, or permanent wait. Either coordinate protocol reset across both ends or use an explicitly designed restart/epoch handshake. Retaining data while forgetting the control state is not sufficient. If the application needs crash-durable exactly-once actions, use appropriate transaction identifiers and commit/recovery semantics.

Lab 5 demonstrates a deliberately simple policy: a failed power/ready contract resets both mailbox endpoints, aborts accepted words that have not yet been delivered, and permits fresh traffic after synchronized release. It tests stopped clocks and restart digitally. It does not simulate a physical power switch, an isolation cell, or analog metastability.

Solved checkpoint — Enable rises at 5 ns while CLK is high from 2–7 ns. When should a low-phase-latch ICG first pass a pulse?

The current 2–7 ns pulse remains blocked because the latch holds its previous zero while CLK is high. The latch can capture enable during the low interval beginning at 7 ns, so the first full permitted pulse begins at 12 ns, assuming the stated 10 ns period and valid enable timing. A direct AND instead creates a 5–7 ns pulse. The ICG still needs its characterized timing margins; this waveform does not establish them.

Check your understanding — a block’s clock is gated off. Has its leakage power stopped?

No. Clock gating stops switching power in the clock tree and the flip-flops, but the transistors stay powered and keep leaking. Removing leakage needs power gating, which brings retention, isolation and restart questions with it.

Key takeaways

  • Clock gating is a timing-sensitive clock function, not an arbitrary Boolean mask.
  • Use a recognized ICG and check enable, test-control, pulse-width, load, and clock-tree requirements.
  • Clock-stop, reset, power-off, retention, isolation, and analog readiness are different states.
  • Keep a powered, clocked path capable of waking the block and completing reset release.
  • Plan cancellation and restart of transactions as carefully as the supply sequence.
