SkyWater 130 nm · Analog, digital, and physical design

Understand the circuit behind the bit.
And the silicon behind the schematic.

A practical course for digital designers learning analog, analog designers learning digital, and everyone who needs to account for the physical chip. It starts with the chip you can hold and ends with the mixed-signal interfaces inside it.

84 illustrated lessons in nine parts7 executable labsOffline reading · Recorded results included

New to chips? Start with Part I

Nine lessons cover the physical chip and the vocabulary every later lesson relies on: what a die, wafer, package and board are, how the layers are built, how a transistor works, which files the design flow produces, and what DC, RMS, decibels and an FFT mean.

  1. What Is a Chip? Board, Package & DieThe nested parts of a finished chip, with real sizes from a SKY130 Caravel die.
  2. The Silicon Wafer: From Crystal to DiesWafers, die grids, scribe lines, yield, dicing, seal rings and shared shuttles.
  3. How a Die Is Built: SKY130 Layers & LithographyFront-end devices, back-end wiring, masks and the official SKY130 layer stack.
  4. The MOSFET: SKY130’s TransistorGate, source, drain and body; NMOS, PMOS, threshold, wells and SKY130 device names.
  5. Anatomy of a Die: Core, Pad Ring & Seal RingWhat sits where on a finished die, using the Caravel harness as the example.
  6. Packages: Wire Bonds, Flip-Chip, QFN & WLCSPHow a die is protected and connected, and what each connection adds electrically.
  7. The PCB and the Path to the TransistorBoard layers, footprints, escape routing, decoupling and the complete supply loop.
  8. The Design Flow and Its Files: RTL to GDSPDK, netlists, Liberty, LEF, DEF, GDS and the checks that come before tapeout.
  9. Signals Primer: DC, AC, RMS, Decibels & the FFTUnits, sine waves, RMS, decibels, time constants, spectra and what an FFT computes.

How to read the numbers. General circuit models, published library data, and illustrative calculations are labeled separately. Exact SKY130 facts cite a device/library/view and corner where relevant. Hand-drawn layouts are teaching diagrams unless explicitly identified otherwise. Use the installed PDK, current project rules, extracted models, and qualified checks for design decisions.
