1. The layers below met1

Lesson 10 showed the inverter as a schematic; lesson 15 showed everything above M1. This lesson fills the gap: the handful of layers that actually form the transistors. Top-down, a standard cell is drawn with:

n-well diffusion (active) poly (gate) contact met1

Active identifies the silicon regions in which source/drain and channel structures are formed; implants and wells determine device type. Poly crossing active forms the illustrated gate. The first diagrams collapse the contact stack into one symbol. In SKY130 it is diffusion/poly → licon1 → li1 → mcon → met1. PMOS devices sit in n-well; ordinary core NMOS devices use p-type body regions. Deep-well/isolated structures and device-recognition layers require the PDK rules.

2. Poly crossing diffusion = a transistor

A transistor is formed where a valid gate pattern crosses the appropriately implanted active region. In this planar drawing, L is the gate’s dimension along current flow and W is the channel width perpendicular to it. L is chosen within the device’s allowed range; it need not be minimum. A process name such as “130 nm” or “5 nm” is not a universal literal gate-length value. The SKY130 HD transistors below have drawn L = 0.15 µm. Drawn and effective electrical lengths also differ.

3. Building the inverter, layer by layer

5. How to read a cell layout

A repeatable order, much like the netlist method from lesson 10:

  1. Find the wells and device markers. In this simple core inverter the upper n-well contains PMOS and the lower p-type body contains NMOS. Well membership alone does not identify every possible PDK device.
  2. Find the gates. Every poly-over-diffusion crossing is a transistor. Count them — you immediately know how complex the cell is (an inverter has 2, a NAND2 has 4).
  3. Follow diffusion and its connections. A region shared by two gates connects their terminals. It forms the series node in the illustrated stack; in folded parallel devices a shared region can be a common source or drain. Trace all contacts before deciding the topology.
  4. Follow M1. Contacts + M1 tell you what connects to what: straps to the rails are supply connections, the strap joining P-side and N-side drains is the output, and isolated M1 landing on poly is an input pin.

Well/substrate taps connect body regions to the intended bias supplies. Some libraries integrate taps in ordinary cells; the HD flow used here relies on dedicated tap cells at controlled spacing. The device type, body domain and isolation strategy determine the connections. See lesson 48 for latch-up and tap placement.

6. The same inverter in the SkyWater library

Figure 3 draws the pins on met1, which is the usual stack for a first layout. sky130_fd_sc_hd__inv_1 puts the signal pins on li1, the local interconnect under met1. The LEF outline is SIZE 1.380 BY 2.720, site unithd, three sites. The SPICE netlist is two transistors, both 0.15 µm long: one NMOS, nfet_01v8, 0.65 µm wide, and one PMOS, pfet_01v8_hvt, 1.0 µm wide. The PMOS is wider than the NMOS, which is what the figure shows, and it is the high-Vt device. The ratio is 1.0 / 0.65 = 1.54.

Pin A is one rectangle, x from 0.32 µm to 0.65 µm and y from 1.075 µm to 1.315 µm, so 0.33 µm by 0.24 µm. Its ANTENNAGATEAREA is 0.2475 µm². That is the gate area of the two transistors: (0.65 + 1.0) × 0.15 = 0.2475. Pin Y's ANTENNADIFFAREA is 0.429 µm². The cell LEF has no OBS block. Nothing in the abstract is declared as a metal blockage over this inverter.

The power pins include both the met1 rails and li1 shapes. VGND has an li1 strip from y = −0.085 to 0.085 µm across the 1.38 µm width, plus a strap toward the NMOS; VPWR has corresponding upper shapes. mcon connects li1 to met1. Lesson 30 discusses current limits and via arrays. This abstract alone does not establish the actual cut count or its allowable operating current.

At the cited tt_025C_1v80 corner, Liberty gives pin A a capacitance of 2.302 fF and pin Y a max_capacitance constraint of 0.181284 pF. The output constraint is not a guarantee of a particular delay or slew at that load. Those electrical models are discussed in lessons 21 and 22; the layout fact here is the layer and shape of each pin.

7. A stick diagram is the sketch before the polygons

Before the rectangles of section 3, the same inverter can be drawn as colored lines. Diffusion runs across the cell. Poly runs from the top diffusion to the bottom diffusion. A dot is a contact. The rails are the two horizontal lines.

In this planar sketch, valid poly/active crossings locate the transistors and contacts determine connectivity. A stick diagram records topology; a physical layout must then satisfy geometry, device-recognition, DRC and LVS requirements. The teaching polygons illustrate that conversion without claiming a passed verification run.

Worked checkpoint — Why are the pin area and gate area different?

The li1 input rectangle has area 0.33 × 0.24 = 0.0792 µm². The connected transistor gates have total drawn area (0.65 + 1.0) × 0.15 = 0.2475 µm², matching the cited antenna gate-area entry. One is the conductor landing shape; the other describes gates connected to that conductor. Neither area alone gives the characterized 2.302 fF input capacitance.

Check your understanding — where exactly is a transistor in a layout?

Wherever poly crosses diffusion. The overlap is the gate; the diffusion on each side forms the source and drain. The drawn W is the width of the diffusion under the gate and L is the width of the poly line: in inv_1, W = 0.65 µm for the NMOS and 1.0 µm for the PMOS, with L = 0.15 µm.

Key takeaways

  • The simplified layer picture introduces active, wells, poly and interconnect; actual device formation also needs the PDK’s implant, isolation and recognition rules.
  • A planar transistor’s drawn L and W follow its gate/active geometry. The process-node name does not specify those dimensions.
  • A continuous gate conductor can join the PMOS and NMOS inputs; real cells may fold devices or choose another physical arrangement.
  • SKY130 uses licon1, li1 and mcon below M1. The verified inv_1 signal pins are on li1, while its row rails are on met1.
  • Shared diffusion is a connection, not proof of a series network. Trace contacts, supplies and gate controls.
  • The checked inv_1 outline is 1.38 × 2.72 µm, with WN = 0.65 µm, WP = 1.0 µm and drawn L = 0.15 µm.
  • A stick diagram establishes intended topology; extracted connectivity and physical checks establish what a completed layout implements.
