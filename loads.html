Loads

What an output has to charge, where that capacitance comes from on SKY130, and why a stronger cell is also a heavier load.

Where the numbers come from. Examples are the open SKY130 high-density library sky130_fd_sc_hd, typical corner 25°C and 1.8 V, unless a corner is named. Capacitance in that library is in picofarads. 0.001 pF is 1 fF.

What you will learn

  • Express an output’s load as receiver pin capacitance plus wire capacitance.
  • Use SKY130 Liberty values such as 2.30 fF for an inv_1 input and 9.00 fF for inv_4.
  • Avoid double-counting a cell’s own parasitics when building a load.

1. The load is the capacitance on the output

A gate does not change its output by magic. The PMOS has to supply current to raise the voltage, or the NMOS has to remove current to lower it. How long that takes depends on how much charge has to move. The thing that stores the charge is a capacitor. That capacitor is the load.

A simple SPICE testbench models the external load as a capacitor from the output to an AC reference. A conventional STA engine uses characterized cell tables plus interconnect models instead of transistor-level simulation on each path. Resistive wires and coupling may require an effective-capacitance model rather than just one lumped C. Larger capacitance normally slows the edge for the same driver and operating condition.

2. On SKY130, a gate input is a few femtofarads

The smallest high-density inverter is sky130_fd_sc_hd__inv_1. Its input pin A is listed as capacitance : 0.002302, which is 2.30 fF. Four of those inputs are 9.21 fF.

The wire adds more. In the technology LEF for this library, minimum-width metal 1 (0.14 µm wide) is about 0.085 fF per micrometer, counting the area of the wire and its two sidewalls. One inverter input is then the same capacitance as about 27 µm of that wire, roughly 59 site widths. A short hop inside a block is a small fraction of a gate. A long route is several gates.

The same input has rise_capacitance : 0.002390 and fall_capacitance : 0.002214 pF. These refer to input transitions at that pin. Bias-dependent device capacitance, switching state and the characterization method can produce a difference; it cannot be uniquely attributed to overlap capacitance from these two numbers alone. The plain value 0.002302 pF is their average in this file.

Metal 1 in sky130_fd_sc_hd.tlef has CAPACITANCE CPERSQDIST 25.7784e-6 pF/µm² and EDGECAPACITANCE 40.567e-6 pF/µm, at a drawn width of 0.14 µm. Per micrometer that is 25.7784e-6 × 0.14 plus two edges of 40.567e-6, which is 0.0847 fF/µm. Sheet resistance on that layer is 0.125 Ω/square, so 0.89 Ω/µm. Twenty-seven micrometers is only about 24 Ω. Against 2.3 fF that RC is a fraction of a picosecond, nothing next to the gate delay in lesson 23.

The local-interconnect layer under it, li1, is the resistive one: 12.2 Ω/square at 0.17 µm wide, about 72 Ω/µm, with a similar capacitance (0.088 fF/µm). Two hundred micrometers of li1 is roughly 14 kΩ and 18 fF, an RC around 250 ps. That is longer than the inverter delay below. On this process a short metal 1 route is a capacitor. A long li1 route is a resistor and a capacitor, and both belong in the load.

The output max_capacitance is 0.181284 pF, about 181 fF. It is an electrical design-rule limit at the named corner, not a promise that any load below it meets a design’s frequency or slew target. Check the characterized table range, output transition limit and actual timing slack too.

3. A stronger cell is a heavier load for whoever drives it

Lesson 11 sized transistors up so the cell could charge a big capacitor faster. Those wider transistors are a bigger capacitor themselves. On this inverter the input capacitance tracks the drive number: inv_2 is 4.46 fF, inv_4 is 9.00 fF, almost two times and four times the 2.30 fF of inv_1.

So the load on a net is a sum you can write down. Add the input capacitance of every pin the net touches, then add the wire. Upsizing the receivers makes that sum larger, which slows the driver that came before them.

The published inverter SPICE calls the SKY130 device subcircuits with w=650000u, w=1e+06u and l=150000u. SPICE’s u suffix evaluates those parameter values to 0.65, 1.0 and 0.15; the called SKY130 wrappers interpret them in micrometers. This is a wrapper convention, not a general rule that a primitive MOS W=0.65 means 0.65 µm in every simulator. The PMOS model is pfet_01v8_hvt, and its width ratio to the NMOS is 1.54.

inv_2 is two of each device. inv_4 is four of each. inv_1 and inv_2 report the same area, 3.7536 µm², which is exactly three sites of 0.46 × 2.72 µm. The second finger fits in the same three sites. inv_4 is 6.256 µm², five sites. Same row height, more sites, as lesson 11 drew.

At an input edge of 0.01 ns and a load of 9.21 fF (four inv_1 inputs), reading in a straight line between the two neighboring load columns:

CellInput capRise delayFall delaySites
inv_12.30 fF71 ps44 ps3
inv_24.46 fF47 ps27 ps3
inv_49.00 fF37 ps20 ps5

Twice the width does not cut the delay in half. Part of the delay is inside the cell and does not shrink with the load resistor. The input capacitance did almost exactly double, and that cost is paid by the previous stage on every transition.

4. Three capacitances, and one number the timer uses

Physically the output sees receiver input capacitances, driver-side diffusion/internal capacitances, and interconnect capacitance. For a standard-cell Liberty lookup, the cell’s own internal parasitics are already represented by its characterization. The external load normally adds receiver pin capacitances and extracted interconnect; do not add the driver’s diffusion again. At transistor level, device models and extracted geometry carry those internal contributions explicitly.

Gate-to-drain capacitance couples an input to a moving output (Miller coupling). Characterized receiver pin capacitance approximates the charge presented to the driver under the characterization conditions. One scalar cannot describe every simultaneous-switching state, nonlinear waveform or coupling event; more detailed timing/noise models or SPICE may be needed. Avoid adding a second arbitrary “Miller capacitor” on top of an already characterized pin model.

Worked check — Four receivers plus 100 µm of minimum met1

Using the quoted nominal estimates, Cpins = 4 × 2.302 = 9.208 fF and Cwire ≈ 100 × 0.084743 = 8.474 fF. External C is about 17.68 fF. Do not add the driver’s output diffusion to this Liberty load. Rwire ≈ 100 × 0.125/0.14 = 89.3 Ω; include its distribution when the required timing/noise accuracy makes it relevant. These isolated-wire LEF estimates omit neighbors and vias.

Check your understanding — a driver feeds two inv_4 inputs over 50 µm of minimum-width met1. Roughly what load does it see?

Two inputs are 2 × 9.00 = 18.0 fF. Minimum met1 is about 0.085 fF/µm, so 50 µm adds about 4.3 fF, for a total near 22 fF. At this length the wire’s resistance hardly matters.

Key takeaways

  • A simple lumped model represents external load as receiver pin capacitances plus wire capacitance. STA uses the applicable load/interconnect model, including distributed RC or coupling when required.
  • sky130_fd_sc_hd__inv_1 pin A is 2.30 fF. Four of them are 9.21 fF.
  • Minimum metal 1 is about 0.085 fF/µm. One inverter input matches about 27 µm of that wire.
  • inv_2 and inv_4 inputs are 4.46 fF and 9.00 fF. A stronger receiver is a heavier load.
  • At the cited nominal corner, inv_1 has max_capacitance ≈ 181 fF. This electrical constraint does not guarantee a required delay or output slew at that load.
  • Avoid double counting: the characterized cell includes its own parasitics, while external load adds receiver pins and interconnect.
