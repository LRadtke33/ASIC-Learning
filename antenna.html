Antenna Diodes

A long metal tied to a gate can charge up during manufacture and punch the gate oxide. A diode gives that charge a path into diffusion. The router adds the diode when the metal-to-gate ratio is too high.

Where the numbers come from. Ratios are the antenna lines in sky130_fd_sc_hd.tlef, read inside the layer they belong to. The diode is the diode_2 LEF, schematic spice, and tt_025C_1v80 Liberty. The flow setting is from the open_pdks LibreLane configuration.

What you will learn

  • Explain what an antenna violation is, and how plasma etch charges metal that is connected only to a gate.
  • State why the damage matters for yield and reliability, and why it is a layout and manufacturing problem, not a functional one.
  • Compute a SKY130 antenna ratio (sidewall vs cut area, per layer and with a diode) from the tech LEF and the official MAX_EGAR / K / diode-bonus table.
  • Choose and describe a fix: a layer jump, a sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2, or a buffer, and how OpenROAD and LibreLane detect and repair violations.

Why it matters
Antenna damage happens during manufacture, before the chip is ever powered.

1. Long metal collects charge, and the gate oxide takes the damage

While a metal layer is being etched, the metal already drawn can collect charge from the plasma. If that metal touches a transistor gate and does not yet touch a diffusion, the charge has nowhere to go except through the gate oxide. A long wire on a small gate is the dangerous case. The check is a ratio: metal connected to the gate, divided by the gate area. If a limit is violated, the flow can add a shunt diode to the same net or change its layer routing. The diode is not inserted in series and does not intentionally interrupt the functional connection. [SKY130 antenna rules]

The gate area the router accumulates is the ANTENNAGATEAREA on the pin. On inv_1 pin A that is 0.2475 µm², which is (0.65 + 1.0) × 0.15 (lesson 16). That number is the gate. It is not a diode.

The charge arrives while the metal is being etched. Plasma in the etch puts charge on every piece of metal that is still floating. If that metal already touches a gate, and the driver at the other end is a higher layer that has not been connected yet, accumulated charge can develop a damaging voltage across the gate oxide. The diode gives it a diffusion path. A jumper up to a higher layer does the other job: during the etch of the lower layer, the long wire is not yet tied to the gate, so only the metal already connected to the gate is counted.

1b. What happens in the process, step by step

An antenna violation is not a functional bug and it is not an ESD event in the finished chip. It is damage that occurs while the wafer is being etched, on a net that is not yet complete. The gate oxide of a MOSFET is a few nanometres of silicon dioxide. Once a poly gate has been patterned, that oxide is a capacitor whose bottom plate is the channel and whose top plate is the poly. Any conductor already tied to that poly, and not yet tied to a diffusion (source, drain, or a protection diode), can collect charge from the plasma used to etch or ash the current layer. If the collected charge raises the voltage across the oxide above its breakdown, a tunnel current flows. The oxide can be punctured, thinned, or left with trapped charge. The finished transistor then has a shifted threshold, higher leakage, or a shorter time-dependent dielectric breakdown (TDDB) life, and the die can fail in test or in the field.

The name “antenna” is the metal itself: during that etch it is a collector for plasma charge, attached only to a small gate. The danger is the ratio of collected area to gate area, not the absolute length. A long, narrow wire on a minimum-size gate is worse than the same wire tied to several gates in parallel, because the charge is shared over more oxide. A connection made on a later layer does not help at this step: that layer has not been deposited yet.

Panel 2 is the violation. The driver at the far end of the net will eventually sit on a higher metal, but that metal does not exist yet. The charge has nowhere to go except through the oxide. Panel 3 is later: a via and a higher metal finally reach a diffusion, and the same net is safe at subsequent etches. Panel 4 is the diode fix: a diffusion is already present at the gate during the early etch, so the ratio is computed with a diode term (section 2b) and the charge has a leakage path into the well or substrate.

1c. Why it matters in layout, and in the factory

From the layout side, every route that ends on a gate is an antenna until a diffusion is attached. Long li1 or met1 runs into a single input pin, large fan-in trees, and scan-enable or reset nets that snake across a block are the usual failures. The check is geometric and is run on the routed database; a clean schematic cannot prove it. A repair that adds a diode adds capacitance (0.878 fF for diode_2 at the typical corner) and a placement site, so timing and density have to be revisited. A repair that jumps the wire to a higher layer changes the resistance and the coupling of that net.

From the manufacturing side, the failure is already in the silicon when the wafer leaves the etch tool. It does not appear in a functional simulation. It reduces yield (a gate that was punctured may fail a voltage-stress or IDDQ screen), and it reduces reliability for gates that were only weakened (a shifted Vt or a shorter TDDB lifetime). The foundry therefore publishes a maximum etch-to-gate area ratio, per layer, and the design cannot be taped out until every pin meets it. The ratios in the next section are those limits for SKY130, taken from the HD technology LEF (what the router reads) and from the official antenna-rule tables (what the foundry checks).

2. The ratios live on specific layers

sky130_fd_sc_hd.tlef does not publish one number for "antenna." Each layer has a piecewise list. The first number in a pair is diffusion area already connected, in µm². The second is the maximum metal area, or metal side area, divided by gate area. A gate that does not yet see any diffusion uses the pair at 0.

Side area is the sidewall of the wire. The plain area ratio, the 3 and the 6, is on the cut layers, not on the metal layers. li1 in this file has a side-area ratio and does not have an area ratio. met1 has a side-area ratio of 400 at zero diffusion and does not have an area-ratio line of its own. The 6 that is easy to quote as "met1" is the via between met1 and met2.

Those first-cell values (the limit at 0 diffusion) are the same numbers the SkyWater antenna-rule page publishes as MAX_EGAR, “maximum etch-gate area ratio without a diode.” The official definitions are in Antenna Rules, Table 83 and Table Ig (the S8P12 / 5-metal stack that matches SKY130). Two kinds of etched area (EA) are used:

  • Vertical, or sidewall, for routing layers. EA = PI × FLT, the perimeter of the interconnect times the final layer thickness. This is what the LEF calls ANTENNADIFFSIDEAREARATIO. For a long, narrow rectangle, PI ≈ 2(L + W) and FLT is the thickness in the LEF (0.10 µm for li1, 0.35 µm for met1 and met2, 0.80 µm for met3 and met4, 1.20 µm for met5).
  • Horizontal, or area, for cut layers. EA = CA, the area of the contact or via. This is ANTENNADIFFAREARATIO. A 0.17 µm-square mcon has CA = 0.0289 µm².

The foundry check is on EGAR, the etch-gate area ratio, against MAX_EGAR for that layer. With no diode,

EGAR = EA / Agate, and the layout must satisfy EGAR ≤ MAX_EGAR.

Agate is the drawn gate area W × L of every MOSFET already connected to the net at that etch, taken from the pin’s ANTENNAGATEAREA. For inv_1 pin A that is 0.2475 µm², which is (0.65 + 1.0) × 0.15. Several gates on the same net add. When a reverse-biased antenna diode is already connected, the official formula is

EGAR = EA / Agate − K × (diode area in µm²) − diode_bonus,

where K and the diode bonus are per-layer constants. The bonus applies once a diode of any size is present. A p+ tap that shorts the net to the substrate (pAntennaShort in the rule text) removes the check entirely; that is a manufacturing short, not a library cell you place for protection.

The LEF stores the same zero-diffusion limits as a piecewise-linear function of already-connected diffusion area, not as a single number. For met1 the four knots are (0, 400), (0.0125, 400), (0.0225, 2609) and (22.5, 11600). Once even a small diffusion is attached, the allowed ratio rises sharply; that is how a diode repairs the violation. The router reads this PWL. The official EGAR formula above is what the foundry deck checks. Use both, and treat a hand calculation that uses only the first knot as a teaching estimate, not as signoff.

The check is per etch, not a single cumulative number for the finished net. During the met1 etch, only metal that already exists and is already tied to the gate is counted — in practice, that met1 plus anything connected downward through already-etched cuts. A long run that will later sit on met3 does not count at the met1 step, and a diffusion that will be attached through met3 does not reduce the met1 ratio. Some flows also accumulate a weighted history across earlier layers; the SKY130 LEF entries used here are per-layer. Always confirm the accumulation rule of the deck you run.

3. The diode the flow inserts is diode_2

sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2 is class CORE, site unithd, SIZE 0.920 BY 2.720, two sites, area 2.5024 µm². One signal pin, DIODE, direction input, on li1. Its ANTENNAGATEAREA is 0. The historical OpenLane configuration used separate cell and pin settings. The current LibreLane configuration stores the pair as sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2/DIODE.

At the typical corner the pin capacitance is 0.000878 pF, which is 0.878 fF. Rise capacitance is 0.000900 pF and fall is 0.000857 pF. Cell leakage is 6.338421×10−6 nW. The schematic is one device, sky130_fd_pr__diode_pw2nd, between VNB and DIODE. The p= and a= suffixes on that line were not converted. This page does not quote a diode area in µm² from them.

For a digital route, 0.878 fF is the cost of fixing the ratio, next to the pin cap in lesson 21. For an analog net, this is a voltage-dependent junction with leakage as well as capacitance. Review whether insertion is acceptable for the signal range, bias, noise, matching, and settling budget; a single Liberty capacitance is not its complete analog model.

The li1 side-area line is PWL ( ( 0 75 ) ( 0.0125 75 ) ( 0.0225 85.125 ) ( 22.5 10200 ) ). met1 through met5 share PWL ( ( 0 400 ) ( 0.0125 400 ) ( 0.0225 2609 ) ( 22.5 11600 ) ). Once some diffusion is already on the net, the allowed ratio climbs. The diffusion-dependent allowance represents the protective effect of connected diffusion; these are two descriptions of the same protection, not two independent bonuses to multiply together. This page does not compute a new ratio from diode_2's diffusion, because the diode area was not converted from the spice suffixes.

mcon area ratio: PWL ( ( 0 3 ) ( 0.0125 3 ) ( 0.0225 3.405 ) ( 22.5 408 ) ). The via cuts use PWL ( ( 0 6 ) ( 0.0125 6 ) ( 0.0225 6.81 ) ( 22.5 816 ) ). There is no separate gate-only ANTENNAAREARATIO on these layers in this tech LEF. The keywords present are the two diffusion ratios above.

Some historical flows used a fake-diode placeholder before replacing it with a real diode. The current cited config names the real diode_2. A placement placeholder alone does not provide physical protection.

In the open SKY130 flow. OpenROAD’s check_antennas computes each net’s antenna ratios after global routing and again after detailed routing. repair_antennas fixes violations by adding diode_2 cells beside the affected gates or by moving wire to a higher layer. LibreLane runs these as CheckAntennas and RepairAntennas, with an optional step that adds diodes in advance; Magic and KLayout check antennas again at signoff.

check_antennas -verbose
repair_antennas sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2

4. A jumper moves the long wire to a later etch

The checking model follows manufacturing layer order and the rule’s partial or cumulative ratio definition. A final-layout net can consist of disconnected pieces at an earlier process step. Metal already connected to the gate counts. Metal that will connect through a via that does not exist yet does not count.

A jumper uses that. The long run is drawn on the next metal, and only a short stub on the lower metal touches the gate. During the lower etch the antenna is the stub. The long wire joins later, when a diffusion connection already exists somewhere on the net, or when diode_2 has been inserted. The entire modified route must still pass the applicable layer rules. Moving a long route upward does not automatically make its later etch safe. OpenROAD’s repair flow supports diode and jumper repairs.

4b. How OpenROAD and LibreLane detect and repair antennas

The open flow checks and repairs antennas after routing, then checks them again at signoff. In OpenROAD the commands are:

check_antennas
repair_antennas sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2
# optional flags: -iterations N    (default 1)
#                 -ratio_margin M  (0 to 100, extra margin on the ratio)
#                 -jumper_only     (layer jumps only, no diodes)
#                 -diode_only      (diodes only, no jumps)

repair_antennas inspects the global-route guides, inserts jumpers and/or diodes near the gates of violating nets, and iterates. Both methods are applied unless a flag restricts the command to one of them. Fillers must not be in the database when it runs; if any cell is inserted, fillers have to be placed again. If the LEF antenna properties are a constant rather than a PWL, inserting a diode does not change the ratio and OpenROAD reports WARNING GRT-0243 and skips diode repair. The SKY130 HD tech LEF uses a PWL, so diode repair is available. If no diode cell is given, OpenROAD looks for a LEF cell of class CORE, ANTENNACELL.

LibreLane’s Classic flow runs this sequence (step names from the built-in flow list):

  1. OpenROAD.GlobalRouting
  2. OpenROAD.CheckAntennas
  3. OpenROAD.RepairDesignPostGRT
  4. Odb.DiodesOnPorts — optional diodes on every port, a preventive strategy
  5. Odb.HeuristicDiodeInsertion — optional diodes on nets that look at risk
  6. OpenROAD.RepairAntennas — the repair_antennas command, typically with sky130_fd_sc_hd__diode_2
  7. OpenROAD.DetailedRouting
  8. OpenROAD.CheckAntennas-1 — a second check on the detailed route
  9. at stream-out, Odb.CheckDesignAntennaProperties and the Magic / KLayout decks

A third structural fix, not done by repair_antennas itself, is to insert a buffer. The buffer places a new gate and a new diffusion (the buffer’s output transistors) in the middle of the long net, so each side is a shorter antenna. It also changes delay and area, so it is a design-repair choice rather than a pure antenna repair.

After any of these fixes, re-run the check. A jumper that clears the met1 ratio can still fail at met2. A diode that clears the ratio adds 0.878 fF and must be extracted and timed. Section 5 works a ratio by hand so the report line has a number you can reconstruct.

5. Polarity and a hand calculation

The HD schematic connects the p-side of diode_pw2nd to VNB and the n-diffusion side to DIODE. With VNB at ground and an ordinary positive signal, that junction is reverse biased. It is not a forward-biased short from a logic 1 to ground. The antenna rule characterizes its manufacturing protection; it is not an ESD or overvoltage qualification for arbitrary inputs.

For an isolated teaching rectangle on met1, assume L = 100 µm, W = 0.14 µm, t = 0.35 µm (the LEF thickness and the official FLT), and one gate area of 0.2475 µm² (inv_1 pin A). Ignoring connected pin metal and other branches, the sidewall area is EA = 2(L + W)t = 70.098 µm². The ratio is EGAR = 70.098 / 0.2475 = 283.2, below the zero-diffusion met1 limit of 400. At L = 200 µm, EA = 140.098 µm² and EGAR = 566.1, which exceeds 400. The same 200 µm run is the left-hand side of Figure B.

The longest isolated met1 rectangle that still passes, for this gate and this width, is the L that gives EGAR = 400:

2(L + 0.14) × 0.35 / 0.2475 = 400 → L = 141.3 µm.

That is a teaching length. The official table’s “max length of Met1 ≈ 570 µm” is for a 1 µm² gate at minimum width, which is 1 / 0.2475 ≈ 4 times more gate area, and 4 × 141 ≈ 564 µm, in agreement. Several input pins on the same net add their gate areas and raise the allowed length.

Now attach a diode and keep L = 200 µm, still with no other diffusion. The official formula for met1 is EGAR = EA/Agate − K × (diode area) − 2200, with K = 400. Even a very small diode makes the second and third terms dominate: EGAR = 566.1 − 400 × Adiode − 2200, which is negative for any positive diode area, so the check passes by a wide margin. That is why a two-site diode_2 is an effective repair, and why the LEF PWL allows a much higher ratio once a few hundredths of a square micrometre of diffusion are connected. These two calculations ignore via cuts, other branches, and pin metal; they show the arithmetic, they are not a signoff result.

Source integration detail: the pinned upstream diode_2.lef lists zero gate area but does not list an ANTENNADIFFAREA for DIODE. A production flow may use a generated or patched abstract. Inspect the installed diode class and diffusion area used by the router; zero gate area alone is insufficient to reproduce diode-based repair. [upstream LEF] [checker model]

Analog ↔ digital: at a net whose original load is an assumed 2.0 fF, adding the TT example’s 0.878 fF increases that lumped load by 43.9%. Digital timing and analog settling both need to be re-evaluated after repair.

Check your understanding — Does adding a diode divide the signal path into two nets?

No. It is a branch from the protected signal to a junction/body connection. The original driver and receiver remain on the same functional signal net.

Check your understanding — a long met1 wire reaches only a gate when met1 is etched; it gains a diffusion connection later through met3. Is the met1 etch still a risk?

Yes. Antenna ratios are checked per etch step. During the met1 etch no diffusion is connected yet, so all of that met1 area counts against the gate. A connection made on a later layer does not help at that step; a diode at the gate, or a jumper that moves the long run to a later layer, does.

Key takeaways

  • Antenna charge on a gate, with no diffusion yet, is limited by a metal-to-gate ratio in the tech LEF.
  • At zero diffusion, li1 side-area ratio is 75. met1 through met5 are 400. mcon area ratio is 3. The via cuts are 6.
  • The area ratio of 6 is on the via layer, not on met1. met1's own line is the side-area ratio.
  • The flow inserts diode_2, two sites, gate area 0, pin capacitance 0.878 fF, one diode_pw2nd.
  • inv_1 pin A at 0.2475 µm² is the gate being protected. It is not the diode.
  • Antenna ratio is per etch, not one number for the finished net. A jumper keeps the long wire on a later metal; a buffer splits the net; diode_2 is the diode this flow inserts.
  • With a diode, EGAR = EA/Agate − K × diode area − bonus. For met1, K is 400 and the bonus is 2200 (SkyWater Table Ig).
  • OpenROAD check_antennas / repair_antennas insert jumpers and diodes; LibreLane runs CheckAntennas, optional DiodesOnPorts and HeuristicDiodeInsertion, RepairAntennas, then CheckAntennas again after detailed routing.
  • The damage is done at etch: a punctured or weakened gate is a yield and reliability (Vt shift, TDDB) problem, invisible to functional simulation.
